# Steam gameplay sky palette and submerged dawn reference

This contract covers the ordinary Steam Direct3D world frame, its sky and geometry fog inputs, and the screen-space background fragment. RVAs are hexadecimal and relative to the preferred base 0x140000000. The locally installed Steam cubeworld.exe, 5,939,072 bytes, has SHA-256 A1EFBBD41EEB8DF08395C4D6511194D416CB70A14054B2A2000B9F5CDBCFA65B. The original unpacked analysis image has SHA-256 7AF18CB28C8BF848F355287428C6390B2EA2A682CA466190F5EB9859C5D6F244 at the same base. The scoped original instruction and function exports identify that unpacked image. The clock divisor, the neutral upper twilight endpoint, and the full sky and water shader blobs used below match byte for byte at their RVAs in the installed image and unpacked image. CPU instruction addresses refer to the unpacked image. The evidence consists of original CPU instructions, binary constant data, call and binding paths, and original shader bytecode. The numeric reference is a static evaluation of those operations.

## Clock and separate daylight values

The world day counter is at byte offset 0x08 and its single-precision time of day is at offset 0x0C. The display at RVA 0x103200 reads that same time field, truncates it to an integer and divides out 3,600,000 milliseconds per hour and 60,000 per minute. The ordinary world update at RVA 0x2DA6C0 calls the clock advance at RVA 0x2E2E80. It adds ten game milliseconds per elapsed real millisecond in the normal case. If the update finds an entity with a zero control byte at offset 0x60, kind 88 at offset 0x68, and a positive floating-point value at offset 0x180, it uses 100 game milliseconds per real millisecond. A null world field at offset 0x210 makes the update set the time field to 32,400,000, equivalent to 09:00. Each time the value is strictly greater than 86,400,000, the advance subtracts that amount and increments the day. An exact value of 86,400,000 can remain for that update. The day field is separate from the phase used by the palette.

The ordinary application frame at RVA 0xA5A50 calls the world renderer at RVA 0xE2E00 from RVA 0xAD9D4. The renderer calls the palette evaluator at RVA 0x2B0400 from RVA 0xE33FA. At RVAs 0x2B047E through 0x2B04EE, the evaluator doubles the time, divides by 86,400,000, and subtracts one in single precision. It takes the absolute value of that signed coordinate and raises it to the third power. Subtract the result from one and raise the remainder to the fourth power to obtain the palette daylight factor. The power calls return single precision. There is no additional clamp on the palette daylight factor on the supported clock domain. Midnight at either end of that domain gives zero, noon gives one, and 06:00 gives 0.586181640625.

The light-collection control in the same ordinary frame is evaluated separately at RVAs 0xE3011 through 0xE3081. Square the signed clock coordinate, subtract from one, raise to the fourth power, double the result, and clamp to the closed interval from 0.05 to 1. The frame later multiplies that state by one minus 0.75 times the local result of RVA 0x2D9F30 before the ordinary collector at RVA 0xCE5F0. The collector reads the resulting state in light level and eligibility decisions, including the 0.4 comparisons. Thus the collector's square and final scale and clamp produce a different clock response from the palette's absolute cube. The separate entity calculation at RVA 0x2DE4C0 also uses the square followed by fourth power within its own branch and applies its own local light sample. At 05:06, the ordinary frame light-collection control before regional multiplication is approximately 0.40152070, while the palette daylight factor is approximately 0.43023476.

## Palette targets, time stages and boundaries

The first four evaluator results feed the four consecutive four-component vectors of the sky pixel constants, in the order lower sky, upper sky, geometric horizon fog, sun tint. This order follows the frame results at RVA 0xE33FA, the ordinary uploads through RVA 0xC7440, and the reads in shader RVA 0x43AEA0. RGB values here are in the native shader's floating-point scale. The neutral lower, upper and fog endpoints have fourth component one. The neutral sun has fourth component zero at night and one at the other endpoints.

| Neutral endpoint | Lower sky RGB | Upper sky RGB | Fog RGB | Sun RGB |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Night | (0, 0.03, 0.15) | (0, 0, 0) | (0, 0.10, 0.10) | (0, 0, 0) |
| Twilight | (1, 0.50, 0.10) | (0.50, 0.20, 0.80) | (1, 0.50, 0.20) | (1.50, 0.85, 0.25) |
| Intermediate | (1, 0.95, 0.50) | (0.30, 0.50, 1) | (0.70, 0.60, 0.30) | (1.50, 0.85, 0.25) |
| Day | (0, 0.60, 1) | (0, 0, 0.80) | (0, 0.10, 1) | (1.50, 1.30, 0.40) |

The evaluator first modifies its palette endpoints for the region as described below. It chooses the time stage from the same daylight factor for all four vectors at RVAs 0x2B1F8A through the stage blocks beginning near 0x2B2120, 0x2B2418 and 0x2B26A4. Each blend is componentwise, with the complementary share applied to the earlier endpoint.

- At or below 0.20, divide the daylight factor by 0.20. Take the distance of that quotient below one, multiply that distance by four, and subtract from one. Values at or below zero are set to zero. The result is the twilight share over night. The effective start is a daylight factor of 0.15, which itself gives zero share. The twilight share is one at 0.20. At 0.175 it is approximately one half. The fourth component of the neutral sun vector grows from zero to one as the twilight share rises.
- Above 0.20 through and including 0.60, divide the excess above 0.20 by 0.40. This is the intermediate share over twilight.
- Above 0.60 through and including 0.75, divide the excess above 0.60 by 0.15. This is the day share over the intermediate endpoint.
- Above 0.75, use the day endpoints directly.

The comparisons and arithmetic use the binary32 constants. An incoming factor exactly equal to binary32 0.60 still selects the twilight-to-intermediate stage and can yield a share of about 1.000000119. A factor exactly equal to 0.75 still selects the intermediate-to-day stage with a share of about 0.999999821. Both latter stages use their division result without a final clamp. The ascending crossings of 0.15, 0.20, 0.60 and 0.75 are around 03:19:33.73, 03:41:49.03, 06:04:58.54 and 07:04:07.48 on the continuous clock. The comparisons govern the boundaries in game, with a few milliseconds of quantization around those crossing times. These stages use the same endpoints on the rising and falling halves of the day.

## Regional inputs and endpoint changes

The evaluator takes signed fixed-point camera coordinates with 65,536 subdivisions per block. Its horizontal inputs for terrain and regional query RVAs 0x358E0, 0x35CE0 and 0x35EA0 are the whole horizontal block counts truncated toward zero. RVA 0x358E0 finds the three-by-three array of neighboring large-region sites around the noise-displaced position, returning site types, horizontal squared distances and the global minimum squared distance. The site position and type calls are RVAs 0x2B9210 and 0x2B9080. RVA 0x35CE0 supplies a local subtype and its falloff. It uses the floor-divided 1,024-block grid and its local site, with an initial radial gate of 282 or 532 according to the local site flag. After that gate the influence is zero outside the corresponding 250- or 500-block warped distance. Inside, it squares the remainder after dividing the warped squared distance by the selected radius squared and subtracting from one.

For the influence of any set of large-region types, find the closest site from that set among the nine samples. A missing type gives zero influence. For a found site, reduce one by the squared-distance excess above the closest site of any type, scaled by 0.0004 twice, and bound at zero. The modifiers below use this distance-relative influence.

- RVA 0x2B5960 starts from one half plus 0.35 times its own horizontal native noise sample. It adds 1.5 times the influence of types 4 or 8, subtracts 0.4 times that of type 10, and subtracts twice that of types 9 or 13. Bound to zero through one. RVA 0x2D9B60 starts from one half plus 0.35 times a separate horizontal noise sample. It adds twice the influence keyed by types 4, 9, 10 or 13 and subtracts the influence keyed by types 6 or 11. Before that combination, local subtype 19 adds its full local weight to the former influence, while 14 or 18 adds one quarter of it. All three local subtypes reduce the latter influence by its local-weight fraction. Bound the combined result to zero through one.
- When the terrain elevation from RVA 0x35EA0 is below 20, both scalars are multiplied by elevation divided by 20 with a floor of zero. At exactly 20 the scalars are unchanged. If the first scaled scalar exceeds 0.50, the day endpoints receive additive RGB offsets with weight twice the excess. The offsets are (0, 0, -0.20) for lower sky, (0, 0.50, -0.30) for upper sky, and (0, 0.50, -0.35) for fog.
- When the second scaled scalar exceeds 0.80 and the first is strictly below 0.40, the three day endpoints move toward (0.60, 0.80, 1), (0.10, 0.40, 1), and (0.80, 0.50, 0.70), respectively. Take the excess of the second scalar above 0.80, divide by 0.20, and multiply by four and the remainder of one minus the first scalar divided by 0.40. Cap that share at one. At either activation equality the modifier is inactive.
- RVA 0x2B9750 returns the influence of large-region type 13. Local subtype 18 adds double the local weight and subtype 19 adds the full local weight, up to one. This supplies the first endpoint replacement table below. RVA 0x2D9F30 supplies the second, using large-region types 5 or 12 and adding the full local weight for subtype 26, up to one. The second acts on the result of the first. Finally local subtype 27 supplies the third table from its local weight. Each replacement runs only when its weight strictly exceeds 0.001. Each listed vector is the endpoint at full replacement. Other endpoint shares remain proportional to the remaining weight.

Local subtype 27 leaves the night endpoints at their preceding values. Its intermediate lower-sky replacement uses the local weight as the replacement fourth component, so the replacement's contribution to that component is the weight squared. The ordinary sun RGB endpoints above are set by daylight stage, outside these regional endpoint tables. A separate special-state gate at RVA 0x2B8050 can alter the palette before selecting the stage. The neutral reference assumes this gate is inactive.

## Camera voxel and frame state

At RVAs 0xE3389 and 0x2B3C5F, the frame and evaluator each pass the same full camera position to RVA 0xEAB0. The three axes are floor-divided by 65,536 into voxel coordinates, including the negative-coordinate correction. One fixed-point unit below an integer plane selects the preceding block. At the plane itself the higher-coordinate block begins. The returned six-byte block record supplies its type at offset 4. The medium branch is taken exactly for type 2. The flag at offset 5 with mask 0x20 participates separately in local lighting color selection, using the query at RVA 0x2B2A36.

The lookup at RVAs 0xE9A0 and 0xEB90 uses the loaded 64-by-64 horizontal chunk and the corresponding column. With no such chunk it returns the default record of type 1. Below a present column's allocated starting height it also returns type 1. For the implicit upper extension of a valid column, positive integer height returns default type 0 and nonpositive integer height returns default type 2. Thus for a column in that extension the height interval beginning at zero still counts as the submerged voxel, and the interval beginning at one counts as dry. Explicit column entries can change that result. The rule is a voxel sample at the render camera, with no continuous animated surface-height comparison in this branch.

After the regional and time blends, the submerged branch at RVA 0x2B3C64 copies the upper-sky target into both the lower-sky and fog targets, including their fourth components. The source vector for both copies is the already evaluated upper target. The target for the sun tint remains the time-stage result. The late player effect lookup at effect ID 19 runs after this substitution. Its share is the lesser of one and the result of subtracting the stored timer from 60,000 and dividing by 3,000. Its sky effect pulls the lower and fog vectors toward (0.90, 1, 1, 1) and the upper vector toward (0, 0, 0, 1). The reference excludes this effect.

In the ordinary frame, the four instantaneous targets are used to update persistent renderer colors before upload. The four-component state at offset 0x4EC is cleared at RVA 0xECE02 and may be set by a separate environment override. The frame compares it component by component with zero at RVA 0xE35F1. With the override zero, lower and upper RGB and their fourth components move toward their target with rate 0.001, fog with rate 0.0005, and sun with rate 0.002. With a nonzero override, lower, upper and fog move toward that override and sun moves toward zero. On that branch both sky rates are 0.001 and both fog and sun rates are 0.0005. The ordinary sky constant upload uses the persistent colors at offsets 0x4AC, 0x4BC, 0x4CC and 0x4DC, in that order.

The coefficient routine at RVA 0xA7A0 takes elapsed frame milliseconds and the selected rate. Truncate one hundred times elapsed milliseconds to the sample count. Starting from zero, advance toward the first through last fractions of a unit ramp, one sample at a time, with each update taking the fraction given by 0.02 times the rate. The ramp begins at one divided by the sample count and ends at one. The routine returns the accumulated coefficient as binary32 and returns zero for a nonpositive count. The frame and RVA 0xCB5C0 apply that coefficient to the difference from the current channel to its target and add the result to the current channel. The different rates can leave spatial color variation during transitions even when the three instantaneous targets coincide. The settled values below are limiting targets for a frame held at the specified input, with finite-precision settling depending on frame history and interval.

## Solar projection and screen-space output

The world sun direction at RVA 0xE38B4 uses the same world clock directly. The angle is twice pi times the day's elapsed fraction. Its direction before unit normalization has the sine of that angle as the first component, negative 0.4 times the sine of half the angle as the second, and negative cosine of the full angle as the third. The vertex-lighting upload uses that world direction through RVA 0xC6CE0. The separate projected sun upload at RVA 0xE3AAC passes the world direction in air and the fixed direction (0, 0, -1) when the camera voxel is type 2.

RVA 0xC7650 takes the direction 100 units out, applies the rotational part of the view and then projection, and divides the projected coordinates by clip W. The visibility component is one for depth in the closed interval from zero to one and zero for depth below zero or above one. It uploads projected X, projected Y shifted by the same vertical offset used by the sky gradient, the viewport width divided by height, and that visibility component. The shared vertical shift from RVA 0xC6B10 is 80 minus the supplied view-angle control, divided by 30. Thus the submerged direction alone does not prove that the sun contribution vanishes at all pitches. Projection and position in the fragment determine its footprint.

The ordinary frame issues the background pass at RVA 0xE3AD4 when display flag 0x80 is clear. The Direct3D table at RVA 0x4385E8 resolves this call to RVA 0xC5120, which selects mode 8. The mode dispatch at RVA 0xC3DC0 and shader binder at RVA 0xC5F80 select pixel shader RVA 0x43AEA0, created at RVA 0xC6273. Vertex shader RVA 0x4387B0 supplies projected coordinates. For the screen-space background, the following order is observable.

1. Divide interpolated clip X and Y by clip W and add the vertical shift to Y. The initial upper-sky share is the Hermite smoothstep of the clamped result of multiplying this Y by 0.4 and adding 0.2. The ramp's input saturates at zero for Y at or below -0.5 and at one for Y at or above 2. Blend lower sky toward upper sky with that share.
2. Form the horizon reference from 60 percent of the fog RGB and 40 percent of the lower-sky RGB. Blend that reference toward the initial sky gradient using the Hermite smoothstep of the clamped result of doubling the shifted Y and adding 1.5. The ramp's input saturates below or at -0.75 and above or at -0.25. For either Hermite smoothstep, square its unit input and multiply by three minus twice that input.
3. Measure horizontal distance from the sun center in projected coordinates and multiply by the uploaded aspect ratio. Measure the vertical distance in the shifted coordinates. Add the two squared components. Multiply that sum by four, cap at one, and subtract from one. Multiply by sun visibility, then raise to the fourth power. That is the share of sun tint over the prior sky. At an aspect-adjusted radius of one half or farther the sun share is zero. Clamp the combined RGB channels to the closed unit interval.
4. Compute luminance from the clamped RGB with weights 0.299, 0.587 and 0.114. Leave the clamped red unchanged in this step and replace green and blue by 65 percent of that channel plus 35 percent of the luminance. Apply the same Hermite smoothstep to each resulting channel independently. The shader writes alpha one.

The screen-space shader reads horizontal and vertical projected position, the four palette vectors, the view-angle shift and projected sun. Geometry fog distance is absent from its bindings. The equal-color result of submerged substitution removes both angular ramps in a fully settled frame, before the projected sun mix and grade.

## Geometry fog and water controls

The frame uploads the submerged-camera material multiplier (0.50, 0.50, 1, 1), with (1, 1, 1, 1) used for the other medium branch. Geometry fog distance at RVA 0xE3A18 uses the scene render distance, which is maintained separately by the frame and its preprocessing at RVA 0xECDA0. The type-2 camera branch takes the lesser of that distance and 150 world blocks. At exactly 150 the selected distance is 150. RVA 0xC6BB0 sends its reciprocal as the fog scale for a strictly positive distance. The world front and back lighting and ambient vectors are other palette outputs, sent through RVA 0xC6CE0 independently of the four pixel palette vectors.

The ordinary water geometry pass at RVA 0xE85E0 selects mode 9. Its binder at RVA 0xC5FF0 selects pixel shader RVA 0x43B790. The same four color vectors and angular sky inputs are available to that shader, together with the geometric fog scale and a separate opacity control. Its local water shade uses vertex lighting and a view-dependent reflection term. It moves this local water color toward the fog RGB based on camera-relative geometric distance. The distance input is squared length multiplied by the squared fog scale, raised to the exponent obtained from one minus 0.75 times the base-two exponential of negative 403.954620 times the fog scale. It caps the result at one and applies Hermite smoothstep. The cube of that smoothed distance share then moves this result toward the angular sky with the projected sun. The final RGB saturates and takes the same red-preserving luminance step and cubic grade. Its alpha starts at 0.75, increases with squared camera distance scaled by 0.000025, caps at one and is multiplied by the pass opacity. These distance and opacity controls belong to the geometry pass. The uniform screen background reference below has no water surface geometry in the fragment.
