# Steam water mesh-group publication and lifetime — 2026-11-07

The ordinary native path builds replacement groups in a staging list, assigns their pointer values into an active list under a world-owned critical section, and retires the prior groups afterward. It does not swap the two list sentinels. The worker publishes only a nonempty whole staging list. An empty water index stream within a group is different: that group can still be published because it has a shared vertex buffer, but the water draw skips it before its per-group WORLD and per-zone point-table submissions.

This bounded source-only contract covers original zone construction RVA 0x2F9400, mesh-container construction 0xC25C0, meshing 0xE9530, publication worker 0x842D0, active water drawing 0xE85E0, and the directly necessary original list, buffer, destruction, batch-dispatch and guard callers. Other mesh families are mentioned only where they share a group, its resources, or its work cost. Their geometry generation is outside scope. Earlier held contracts, including the shader and pass-state documents, remain unchanged.

Water traverses the renderer's zone-pointer span at renderer +0x300 through +0x308 in each of its two sweeps. Its full upstream population policy is outside this contract. For each supplied zone, it first tests the active count at zone +0x1D0, then traverses the active sentinel at +0x1C8 and its nodes in list order. It never draws from +0x1D8 here. The first sweep's count, list and water-wrapper gates are at 0xE86A3, 0xE86B1 and 0xE86D4; the corresponding later sweep water-wrapper gate is 0xE8884.

An empty active list produces no group visit, so there is no per-group WORLD submission and no per-zone point-table submission from either group traversal. A nonempty active group whose water index wrapper at +0x18 is null is also skipped before both effects. In ordinary construction, an empty water index vector causes precisely that null-wrapper case, even when a shared vertex wrapper and other index streams keep the group present in the active list. This differs from a wholly empty replacement staging list: publication skips the latter, preserving previous active groups on this worker path. An accepted replacement containing other geometry but no water can still replace and retire the previous water-bearing active groups, after which the water traversal skips those new groups.

For a nonnull water wrapper, the first sweep submits the group's WORLD matrix at 0xE87B9, then invokes the shared vertex wrapper's bind slot +0x08 at 0xE87C3, and only if that returns true invokes the water index wrapper's bind slot +0x08 at 0xE87D1. A true second result permits the draw at 0xE87EF. In the later sweep, the zone's point-table inputs at zone +0x240 and +0x340 are submitted for each eligible group at 0xE88A3, before that group's WORLD call at 0xE8983; the bind and draw sites are 0xE898D, 0xE899B and 0xE89B9. The point-table call is repeated per eligible group and is not made for every zone irrespective of its groups. The initial zero-point-table setup at 0xE8658 precedes the traversals and is a separate routine-level call.

Accordingly, a false bind result would skip the draw after WORLD, and after the later sweep's point-table submission. In the concrete original D3D11 wrappers, however, vertex bind routine 0xC2A70 and index bind routine 0xC2A40 both submit their context binds and return true. The vertex bind uses stride eight and zero offset; the index bind uses its stored format value and zero offset. Water has no explicit null check on group +0x08 before dereferencing it; ordinary construction supplies the nonnull shared wrapper through its group-admission rule. It also has no positive-count admission test on group +0x40 or +0x48. The draw receives those 32-bit values; concrete graphics draw RVA 0xC2D80 uses three indices per water triangle, with zero base offsets, and does not use the forwarded shared vertex count as an admission test. Therefore a nonnull water wrapper accompanied by a zero triangle count is not suppressed by a count gate: WORLD, the later sweep's zone tables, binding and a zero-count draw still occur on that path. This is a statement about the accepted state, not a claim that ordinary empty vectors create such wrappers.

The group owns destruction of its resource wrappers. Its table entry at 0x4382B0 selects deleting destructor 0xC2860, which calls 0xC2770 and, when requested, frees the 0x60-byte group. Body 0xC2770 destroys nonnull wrappers in increasing field order +0x08 through +0x38; water-wrapper destruction is at site 0xC27BA. The concrete vertex destructor is 0xC2950 and the concrete index destructor is 0xC28F0. They release the resource at wrapper +0x10 through its release slot at sites 0xC297A and 0xC291A, then free the wrapper when requested. The inspected constructors and publication copies do not add a resource or group reference merely because a raw pointer is placed in another list.

Zone destruction provides the final ownership backstop. Original zone destructor 0x2F9940 calls embedded mesh destructor 0xC2680 at site 0x2F9D5F. It destroys group payloads from the active list first, then from staging, and subsequently frees both lists' nodes and sentinels. This proves destruction policy for the two lists in a settled state; it does not prove that destruction during the temporary pointer-duplication interval is allowed. Which outer thread/guard retires a zone and how outstanding neighborhood snapshots are protected are unresolved here.

Bounds and light coupling remain deliberately narrow. The bounds and +0x444 cost written during staging construction are zone-level state, not members of the pointer sequence copied at publication. Frame helper 0xECDA0, called before water, separately checks existing active count and reads the zone bounds at its 0xED1FE bounds-test call and centers at 0xED20B and 0xED212 in its distance/fog calculation. Thus updated staging-era bounds can affect that shared calculation before the new active groups are installed. Water's later sweep reads the live zone point tables at +0x240 and +0x340; publication transfers group pointers and does not transfer a per-group copy of those tables. Their complete writer and cache-invalidation protocol, and a guarantee that they represent the same geometry generation as the active groups, have not been established. The generated packed vertices already contain the output of the earlier CPU light/packing calls; this does not make the separately supplied point tables group-owned.

Failure and empty results must not be conflated. Missing-zone meshing returns without changing the lists. A normal meshing result with no admitted shared-vertex groups clears and retires old staging, updates bounds/cost/admission state, and leaves staging count zero; this worker then leaves old active groups intact. A water-only empty stream inside otherwise admitted groups follows the earlier null-wrapper rule and can remove previously active water through a nonempty whole-group replacement. Buffer factories do not check the device creation result before constructing their wrappers; wrapper existence and bind returns are not proof of a valid GPU resource. No general rollback, retry, device-error outcome, allocation-failure cleanup guarantee or cancellation outcome is established. Sleep(0), list assignment and the wrapper no-op visits do not prove GPU completion. The bounded audit establishes the ordinary CPU ownership and call order above, without inventing broader synchronization or a universal absence of other writers.
