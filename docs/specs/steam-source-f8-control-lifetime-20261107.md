# Native Steam source byte +0xF8 control lifetime

This bounded audit traces source byte +0xF8 through its directly identified constructor and reset writes, ordinary viewed-World consumption, reached coordinate-message construction and host reply assembly, and the selected retention/restoration routines. It applies to the original x64 Steam image binary/cubeworld.original.exe.unpacked.exe, image base 0x140000000, PE timestamp 0x5D93AB0D, SHA256 7AF18CB28C8BF848F355287428C6390B2EA2A682CA466190F5EB9859C5D6F244. The hash was checked for this audit. Evidence is limited to individually identified original decompiler files and the original decompiled/assembly.txt listing. No business label is assigned from the byte's proximity to other fields. All earlier held contracts remain unchanged.

The three adjacent source bytes have distinct proved accesses. Source construction at RVA 0x2F9400 writes the 16-bit value one at source+0xF8 at RVA 0x2F94FF. In this x64 image that initializes byte +0xF8 to one and byte +0xF9 to zero. The separate byte write at RVA 0x2F9508 initializes +0xFA to zero. Later identified writes to +0xF8 are one-byte operations and do not overwrite the neighboring bytes.

RVA 0x2DA090 walks the loaded source map at World+0x460. For every reached nonnull source it writes one to +0xF8 at RVA 0x2DA0CE before testing the edit-list count at source+0xE0 at RVA 0x2DA0D5. Consequently an existing source with no recorded edits still receives the byte-one write. A null source is skipped. This helper has no internal World+0x358 admission and does not create a missing source.

When that source has a nonempty edit list at +0xD8, the same helper replays its records in reverse list order. The call to RVA 0x31C1C0 at RVA 0x2DA10F receives the record's coordinates and the value beginning at edit-list node+0x1C. This differs from the retained-restoration replay's value beginning at node+0x22. After its reached replay, RVA 0x2DA090 clears and frees the edit-list nodes, writes zero to count +0xE0 at RVA 0x2DA180, sets source byte +0x23C to one at RVA 0x2DA1A4 and records a 32-bit millisecond value at source+0x238 at RVA 0x2DA1B1. Those latter list and mesh-invalidation effects require the nonempty-edit branch; the +0xF8 write does not. The full edit-value producer taxonomy and replay's column admissions are outside this byte-lifetime audit.

A directly identified caller is connection setup at RVA 0x3D820. It obtains the viewed World through its owner+0xA50 Game backpointer and Game+0x8, calls RVA 0x2D2C00 at RVA 0x3DA3C, then calls RVA 0x2DA090 at RVA 0x3DA4C. Later, after its same-identity or different-identity setup branches, it writes one to that viewed World's byte +0x358 at RVA 0x3DB2D. This establishes the order of the F8-setting traversal before that role-gate write for the sources present when the traversal runs. The complete effects of the preceding cleanup and all alternate setup callers are not inferred.

The source objects used by ordinary Game update at RVA 0xA5A50 belong to the World reached through Game+0x8. The host has a separately constructed embedded World at Host+0x8, reached in the ordinary Game layout through Game+0x6F0: host construction is selected at Game+0x6E8 at RVA 0x7EB24, called at RVA 0x7EB36, and constructs its World at RVA 0xF17E1. A viewed-source byte being cleared does not itself clear a host-source byte at the same coordinate. These identities matter even for locally hosted play. World+0x358 is stated below only as the observed zero/nonzero predicate, without a remote-only name.

The viewed coordinate-input pass in Game update processes the collection at Game+0x19E0 when its count is nonzero. For each coordinate it calls the loaded-only getter at RVA 0x16E00, from RVA 0xA821B. A nonnull source receives byte zero at source+0xF8 at RVA 0xA8225, regardless of its prior F8 value. A missing map entry or stored null pointer produces no write. The getter does not insert a source-map entry or invoke generation. This pass has no additional local World+0x358 test in its inspected body. The full production, buffering and later reuse policy of that input collection is not closed here.

Within the inspected Game update, the F8-clear pass precedes the coordinate-and-array input replacements at RVAs 0xA82CF and 0xA8396 and the dirty publisher at RVA 0xA8498. A later F8 consumer occurs at RVAs 0xAADDA–0xAAEBF. These operations lie within the viewed World's World+0x3C8 scope, entered at RVA 0xA6464 and released at RVA 0xADB23. This is the directly reached single-update order, not a claim about every external producer or caller.

At RVA 0xAADDA the update clears the list at Game+0x2240 through RVA 0x3C9C0. It then tests viewed World byte +0x358 at RVA 0xAADE3. Zero skips construction of this coordinate list. Nonzero admits a scan around the resident pointer at World+0x448. The ordinary position components are signed 64-bit fixed positions at resident+0x10 and +0x18, converted by division by 65,536 with truncation toward zero and narrowed to signed 32-bit block components. If resident byte +0x68 equals 0x9C, the signed 32-bit components at +0x1B0 and +0x1B4 are substituted.

The scan obtains inclusive zone bounds for the sampled block components minus and plus 256 blocks, using the signed floor-by-64 helper at RVA 0xBA800 through RVAs 0xAAE2F, 0xAAE3C, 0xAAE4A and 0xAAE58. In the ordinary nonoverflow domain this is a nine-by-nine square of potential coordinates, with X as the outer traversal and Y as the inner traversal. No disk filter appears in this consumer. Extreme signed arithmetic limits and alternative resident meanings are not established here.

For each coordinate, the scan looks up an already loaded source through RVA 0x16E00 at RVA 0xAAE79. It requires a nonnull source and a nonzero source+0xF8 byte, tested at RVA 0xAAE83. On admission it appends that coordinate pair to Game+0x2240 through RVA 0xA1990 at RVA 0xAAEA6. The helper appends to an ordered linked list; it is not the unique source-pointer-set insertion used for dirty arrays. Missing/null sources do not contribute coordinates here, so this particular F8 consumer is not a missing-source generation or prefetch request.

The scan does not clear F8 when adding the coordinate. A surviving source with a nonzero byte can therefore be selected again in a later admitted update while it remains within the scan. An earlier input clear to zero prevents this later scan from selecting that same surviving object in the inspected update unless another intervening writer changes it. With World+0x358 zero, the output list was still cleared but no coordinates were added by this scan. The connection routine at RVA 0x3F4F0 is then called at RVA 0xAAEBF.

The directly reached coordinate-send portion of RVA 0x3F4F0 follows its existing connection identity admissions and timing gates. The inspected path requires unsigned elapsed time from owner+0x9EC to be at most 5,000 milliseconds at RVA 0x3F70F, and more than 32 milliseconds from the module timestamp at image RVA 0x54DE00 at RVA 0x3F881. Within that admitted block, the coordinate portion requires more than 1,000 milliseconds from the separate module timestamp at image RVA 0x54DE08, compared at RVA 0x401F8. It updates that timestamp before traversing the current Game+0x2240 list. The complete identity-bitfield validity policy, timeout handling and scheduling of other callers are outside scope; these gates are not a promised fixed per-source request period.

For each visited coordinate, that sender constructs a message beginning with the 32-bit value 0xF at RVA 0x40258, followed by the eight coordinate bytes, and calls RVA 0x3F460 at RVA 0x402A1. F8 itself is not serialized in this message. The routing helper compares the supplied peer identity with the global identity at image RVA 0x54DDC8. The equal-identity route calls RVA 0xF32C0 when owner+0xA10 is nonnull; the other route invokes the reached outgoing interface. RVA 0xF32C0 copies the message into the host's protected queue at Host+0x620 under Host+0x5D0. Sending or queueing this message does not reset the source byte in the inspected route. Transport success, synchronous processing and exact retry timing are not proved.

The original host message handler at RVA 0xF4490 processes message value 0xF only when the sender already resolves to a nonnull record under Host+0x568. On the well-formed reached route it reads the eight coordinate bytes and appends them to that record's pending coordinate list at record+0x110 through RVA 0xF4420, called at RVA 0xF4DD2. It does not inspect a source F8 byte or create a source in this message case. Host update at RVA 0xF5520 invokes the same handler for both its reached network input and the queued local messages. Malformed or truncated message behavior is not specified by this audit.

The reached state-reply assembly at RVA 0xF33B0 walks that pending coordinate list. It queries the host World's loaded source map at RVA 0xF381F and requires an existing nonnull source. For such a source it appends saved edits when source count +0xE0 is nonzero, admits a first-array snapshot when source+0xF9 is nonzero at RVA 0xF386A, and admits a second-array snapshot when source+0xFA is nonzero at RVA 0xF3946. The corresponding array snapshot calls are at RVAs 0xF3883 and 0xF395B.

After these optional data contributions, the reply builder always appends the requested coordinate to the record's reply coordinate list at record+0x80 through RVA 0xF4420 at RVA 0xF39BD, provided the source lookup succeeded. This coordinate contribution does not require nonempty edits, a selected-array flag or a particular host-source F8 value. The inspected reply builder neither reads nor copies nor clears host-source F8. If the requested source is missing/null, it contributes neither this coordinate nor the source-specific data through that branch. After visiting the pending list, the routine clears that entire list and zeros its count at RVA 0xF39EF, including requests whose source was unavailable. The complete reply serialization, transport and mapping into the viewed Game+0x19E0 collection are not closed here.

Thus viewed F8 can admit a coordinate message which later selects source state for a reply, but the surviving F8 value of the host source does not select the content of this directly inspected reply branch. The viewed input write, the request list, the host pending list and the reply-coordinate list are distinct state. No universal acknowledgement, terrain-validity or readiness label is substituted for their observed operations.

Retention at RVA 0x2D3E20 does not read or store the source's F8 byte among its selected fields. Its two array selectors are the adjacent +0xF9/+0xFA bytes; it additionally copies the reached edit list, count collection and scalar. F8 being zero or nonzero does not itself admit or suppress those selected copies in this body. The retained coordinate record is not a saved copy of the whole physical source object.

The source factory at RVA 0x2AE0C0 returns an existing nonnull loaded source through the fast-return branch at RVA 0x2AE129, without an F8 write on that branch. Its missing/null route constructs a fresh object through RVA 0x2F9400 at RVA 0x2AE16A and runs generation at RVA 0x2AE179. World+0x358 zero then permits selected retained-state restoration; nonzero skips it through RVA 0x2AE186. Neither selected restoration branch restores an old F8 byte. The writes to neighboring +0xF9/+0xFA at RVAs 0x2AE26A and 0x2AE284 are individual byte-one writes. The direct constructor default and absence of a selected old-F8 copy must be distinguished from an exhaustive claim about transitive generation helpers or indirect writers.

The directly established live-source F8 history in this audit is therefore constructor initialization to one, setting to one for every nonnull source visited by RVA 0x2DA090, and clearing to zero for an existing source admitted by the viewed coordinate-input pass. The directly identified read admits loaded coordinates into the later list when nonzero. No additional source-owned F8 access was established from unrelated same-offset matches. Pointer aliases, bulk writes, indirect calls, untraced producer or deserialization paths, all alternate callers and wider lifetime races remain explicitly unresolved. No universal writer census or safety claim is made.
