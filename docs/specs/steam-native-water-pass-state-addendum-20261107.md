This destination-to-slot join is the decisive distinction. Shader-state buffer pointers +0x428, +0x430 and +0x438 bind in that order to vertex slots 7, 8 and 9; their meanings are White, DepthOffset and Glow. Setter address order does not establish that order. In particular, the water call through byte-slot +0x78 reaches the buffer at shader-state + 0x438 and therefore controls Glow. It does not control DepthOffset. Pixel Alpha has a separate object, stage and slot from all three vertex controls.

The four scalar setters compare the incoming float with their respective cached float. An equal, ordered comparison skips the cache write and buffer update; unequal or unordered comparisons take the update path. That path places the supplied float first in a 16-byte payload, with the remaining twelve bytes zero, updates the corresponding cache, and submits that payload to the identified buffer. Thus White-zero, Glow-zero and Alpha requests describe the selected state; they do not imply an unconditional upload when the requested value is already cached. There is no scalar conversion or clamp in these setters. The exit Alpha value is independently fixed by original float data at RVA 0x4AC980, read at site 0xE8685 and supplied at 0xE89DF. It is 1.0, rather than a restoration of an arbitrary incoming Alpha value.

The setup order in RVA 0xE85E0 is exact. Site 0xE8621 selects mode 5 through renderer byte-slot +0x08. White-zero follows at 0xE8630, Glow-zero at 0xE863F, material at 0xE864F, the zero-light helper at 0xE8658, and pixel Alpha from renderer + 0x4A8 at 0xE866C. All precede the first zone/group traversal. After that traversal, site 0xE8816 selects mode 9; VIEW and projection are installed at 0xE8826 and 0xE8836 before the second traversal. The final Alpha request is at 0xE89DF after that traversal. These setup and final calls also occur on ordinary paths where the traversals find no groups to draw. The routine makes no later request to restore an earlier White or Glow value.
