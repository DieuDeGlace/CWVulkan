# Steam cloud motion, underwater visibility and fog

Final / Stable / Held. Scope is the ordinary world-rendering path of the original 64-bit Steam image. The unpacked executable has SHA-256 7AF18CB28C8BF848F355287428C6390B2EA2A682CA466190F5EB9859C5D6F244. All addresses below are relative to that image. Evidence is offline and static.

## Motion and camera movement

An individual cloud stays anchored to its world location as world time advances. On each draw, Steam rebuilds the candidates from a stationary staggered grid. It uses the camera's horizontal position to choose the neighborhood. Crossing a grid boundary can introduce candidates at one edge and drop candidates at the other. Moving or turning the camera changes where an existing cloud projects on screen. Holding the same camera, world inputs and render origin while advancing the clock supplies zero horizontal translation to an existing cloud. Confidence is high for the two ordinary voxel cloud models. Their registry identities are 0xDB8 and 0xDB9, established in routine 0x177F50 at 0x1DEFF4–0x1DF059 and 0x1DF298–0x1DF2FD and used by routine 0xE5730.

The grid has 200-block spacing on each horizontal axis. The center indices come from the camera coordinates converted from 65,536 fixed-point units per block, followed by signed division by 200, truncating toward zero. A column's world X is 200 times its signed index. Its world Y starts at 200 times the row index. A positive odd column adds 100 blocks, a negative odd column subtracts 100, and an even column adds zero. Thus the central row has anchors at (-200, -100), (0, 0) and (200, 100). A positive current viewing range adds one surrounding grid cell for each complete 200 blocks of range, in addition to the two-cell margin on each side. These are candidate locations before the environment, distance and frustum tests. Evidence is in 0xE5730 at 0xE5881–0xE5B50 and in 0xE08C0.

An accepted cloud's height includes the terrain-height sample and a spatial variation evaluated from the grid indices. Scale and quarter-turn orientation are also spatial. The model is centered horizontally using its dimensions after the scale and rotation are selected. The scene subtracts the current camera-follow reference from each horizontal world anchor for rendering. The camera eye and that reference are separate positions supplied by the frame caller. Camera following, orbit and collision adjustment can move the eye relative to the reference. The vertex program projects the model with the supplied world, view and projection transforms. None of these steps adds a clock or wind displacement to the world anchor. Evidence is in 0xADCD0, 0xA5A50 at 0xAD907–0xAD9D4, 0xE2E00 at 0xE2EC2–0xE2F31, 0xE5730 and the vertex program at 0x4387B0.

## Underwater visibility

The camera voxel determines the underwater render state. The scene samples all three camera-eye coordinates, floors them to block coordinates, and reads the material byte at offset four of the result. Material 2 selects the underwater branch. The query accounts for stored column contents and the water fallback for eligible empty cells at or below block Z zero. Camera altitude alone is insufficient to classify a cell. Evidence is in 0xE2E00 at 0xE3384–0xE3393 and in 0xEAB0, 0xE9A0 and 0xEB90.

Submerging the camera preserves eligibility for the ordinary cloud pass. The branch immediately guarding that pass reads bit 0x80 of the incoming scene flags. Clear permits the call and set skips it. The ordinary frame caller sets this bit from a separate global byte at 0x5514D6, which starts zero in the image. The camera-material check affects fog and another earlier rendering operation. It does not supply this bit. Confidence is high for the branch and its caller. The meaning and all possible external writers of the global byte remain unidentified. No runtime claim is made about seeing an individual underwater cloud through water, terrain and fog. Evidence is in 0xA5A50 at 0xAD757–0xAD775 and 0xAD8B6–0xAD915, and in 0xE2E00 at 0xE3ABF–0xE3AED and 0xE3D31–0xE3D65.

## Fog range at the cloud draw

Clouds inherit the range set earlier in the same world draw. The scene reads its current floating-point viewing distance at offset 0x4A4. In air it passes that distance to the fog setter. In camera material 2 it passes the smaller of that distance and 150 blocks. The setter accepts a positive distance, uploads its reciprocal, and retains the existing value for a nonpositive input. An identical reciprocal reuses the existing upload. Cloud mode 10 binds the pixel fog-distance buffer at slot 1. It leaves this range in force for the cloud models. The conditional 100,000-unit fog setting later in the frame occurs after the cloud call. The cloud candidate scan and CPU culling still read the scene's viewing distance at 0x4A4 independently of the 150-block shader cap. Evidence is in 0xE2E00 at 0xE32AB, 0xE3A1B–0xE3A40 and 0xE3D60–0xE3D99, and in 0xC6BB0, 0xC3DC0, 0xC5E50, 0xC68C0, 0xE5730 and 0xE08C0.

The cloud pixel program measures three-dimensional distance from the eye to each shaded point. The fog blend starts at zero at the eye, reaches roughly 3.10 percent at half the bound range, and reaches 100 percent at and beyond that range. More precisely, it clamps the squared distance divided by the squared range to the interval from zero to one, cubes the result, and uses one minus the squared complement of that cube as the fog weight. The target is the directional sky/fog/sun color for that pixel. The distance blend changes color. The drawing pass supplies opacity independently. Confidence is high for this shader and binding. Evidence is in the vertex program at 0x4387B0, cloud pixel program at 0x43CD00, shader creation at 0xC60D0 and binding at 0xC5E50 and 0xC68C0.

These findings cover the original Steam gameplay path and its two cloud models. They do not establish behavior in another game build or a separate weather or particle effect. No original game code, shader listing or pseudocode is reproduced.
