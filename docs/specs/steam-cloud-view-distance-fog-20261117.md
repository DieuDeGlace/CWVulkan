# Steam cloud view distance, fog, and model color

## Evidence and coordinates

The supported Steam image is the installed x64 CubeWorld.exe, 5,939,072 bytes, SHA-256 `a1efbbd41eeb8df08395c4d6511194d416cb70a14054b2a2000b9f5cdbcfa65b`. The original packed file in the export bundle matches that hash. The original unpacked analysis PE is 5,757,952 bytes, SHA-256 `7af18cb28c8bf848f355287428c6390b2ea2a682ca466190f5eb9859c5d6f244`, and matches the export metadata. RVAs below refer to the unpacked image at preferred base `0x140000000`. Evidence is static original CPU instructions, PE data, and embedded shader bytecode.

The camera update at `0xadcd0` builds an orbit eye from the tracked focus, with collision handling. The frame caller at `0xad9d4` supplies the eye from game offsets `+0x1dc0/+0x1dc8/+0x1dd0` and the focus from `+0x1dd8/+0x1de0/+0x1de8`. At `0xe2ec5–0xe2edb` the renderer takes the three signed 64-bit eye coordinates into `+0x338/+0x340/+0x348`. One block is 65,536 fixed-point units. Renderer `+0x428/+0x430` are the negatives of focus X/Y and rebase draw and plane-test positions horizontally. The render view and projection matrices reside at `+0x39c/+0x3dc`. The main frame reaches the cloud pass at `0xe3d60` when frame flag `0x80` is clear.

## Range ownership and option values

The persistent `renderDistance` setting is a signed integer at settings offset `+0x14`. It is loaded at `0x13ac90–0x13ac99` and copied with the settings into the game state at `0x7ea80` (game offset `+0x1b9c`). At `0xad863–0xad8b2` the frame takes the larger of 3 and the integer quotient of raw setting times 30 divided by 100, truncated toward zero. The frame stores that chunk count in renderer `+0x438` at `0xe2fe1`.

The float at renderer `+0x4a4` is the live range R consumed by clouds. The update at `0xecda0`, called at `0xe3096`, starts with the square of 64 blocks times the chunk count. In the camera-centered 64-block chunk scan, missing or unready terrain can lower that squared value to its horizontal squared distance from the eye to the chunk center. The visibility and budget scan records ready terrain entries that pass the bound/plane test at `0xe0680` and whose horizontal squared distance is within the current value. It sorts those entries by squared distance and accumulates their signed counts from terrain-entry `+0x444`. At the first count reaching chunk count squared times 12,288, it substitutes the lesser of that record's squared distance and the preceding record's squared distance, with 0 as the initial preceding distance. It then subtracts the float 44.8 from the square root and floors the target at 0.001. The missing-chunk, budget, and target sites are `0xed014–0xed06e`, `0xed203–0xed268`, and `0xed424–0xed4ae`.

In the ordinary state R drops to a lower target immediately and approaches a higher target with the frame-step response at `0xa7a0` using strength 0.0005. When the target exceeds 256 and the nonzero region-color condition or effect ID `0x13` is active, the same response approaches 256. The actor state byte `0x9c` with the float at actor `+0x6c` above 1,000 instead approaches the greater of the target and 64 with strength 0.005. These are writes to the same R at `0xed618–0xed711`. For the response at `0xa7a0`, the sample count is the frame step times 100 truncated to an integer. Starting at zero, each sample approaches the next evenly spaced fraction of one by the fraction 0.02 times the supplied strength. The resulting float is the blend fraction for the frame update.

## Cloud cells, visibility, and opacity

At `0xe5730`, each base index is the corresponding fixed-point eye component truncated to blocks, then divided by 200 with truncation toward zero. The pass visits X in increasing order outside the Y loop, with both offset ranges inclusive. The lower offset is minus 2 minus R divided by 200 truncated toward zero. The upper offset is 2 minus R divided by negative 200 truncated toward zero. At grid column and row the anchor has X equal to 200 times the column and Y equal to 200 times the row plus 100 times the signed remainder of the column divided by 2. This preserves the sign of staggered rows in negative columns. The cloud eligibility comparison uses the local region result at `0x2b5960` and two-dimensional noise at `0x2e0c0` sampled at the anchor X/Y. The pass adds 1 to the noise, halves it, and accepts when that value is at most half the region result. The anchor's fixed-point Z is the nonnegative, truncated fixed-point height from `0x35ea0` with selector 1, plus the truncated fixed-point value of 200 blocks plus 30 times noise at 17 times column and 19 times row. The range test receives this anchor before centering the geometry.

At call site `0xe5aca`, the position test `0xe08c0` receives radius 250 for the planes and R plus 200 for the range. The range stage squares each signed fixed-point displacement from the eye separately, scales each square down by 65,536 with integer truncation, and sums. It accepts the inclusive threshold obtained by squaring the single-precision sum of R and 200, multiplying that square by 65,536, then truncating. The plane stage converts the anchor to blocks after adding the horizontal rebase, keeps absolute Z, and rejects if any of the six plane evaluations plus 250 is below zero. The six float4 planes at `+0x43c` are extracted at `0xdf8f0` from the combined view/projection coefficients as W plus or minus each of X, Y, and Z. The native test uses the coefficients as stored and accepts equality.

After visibility and the world transform update, `0xe5f16–0xe5ff7` measures 3D Euclidean eye-to-anchor distance again using float block displacements. Squared distance of 32,400 or more draws immediately at alpha 1. For closer anchors it subtracts 80 blocks from the distance, multiplies the difference by the float 0.01, and clamps to zero through one. Alpha below 0.01 skips the draw. Alpha from 0.01 inclusive to 1 exclusive queues the transform and model in traversal order. At `0xe6152–0xe61bf` the pass first draws every queued model at alpha zero, then draws each again at its saved alpha. Mode 10 uses a depth-writing less-or-equal test and source-alpha RGB blending, so the zero-alpha draws still affect depth. The opacity setter is `0xc6790`, which updates pixel buffer 4. The near band is approximately 81 through 180 blocks and is evaluated from the original anchor. Distant pixels still use shader fog up to the independently tested positional limit.

## Active fog shader and producer

The selector at `0xe57e0` enters renderer mode 10. The D3D renderer table at `0x4385e8` points to `0xc3dc0`. Mode 10 invokes `0xc5e50`, which binds the vertex blob at `0x4387b0` and the pixel blob at `0x43cd00` using objects created at `0xc60d0`. At `0xc68c0`, pixel buffer 1 comes from shader-state offset `+0x448`. The frame writes that buffer before the clouds at `0xe3a40` through renderer virtual slot `+0x80`, thunk `0xc59e0`, and setter `0xc6bb0`. For positive input the setter writes its float reciprocal when it differs from the cached scale. The normal input is the current R at renderer `+0x4a4`. The frame's camera medium class 2 instead supplies the smaller of R and 150. The cloud pass binds the buffer and inherits the frame's value. Its additional 200-block range allowance enters only the center-distance test.

The cloud vertex shader passes the transformed view-space vertex position as the XYZ varying read by the active pixel shader. The pixel shader takes the squared length of that interpolated vector, multiplies by the squared scale in pixel buffer 1, and saturates to zero through one. It cubes this saturated value using the two complement operations around the cube. The resulting weight interpolates processed vertex RGB toward directional sky/fog RGB before final color shaping. Its source alpha is the independent float in pixel buffer 4. The normal fog distances in the table thus refer to view-space vertex lengths at saturation, whereas the center-distance test uses absolute eye and anchor positions.

## Original model-face light and vertex color

The selector at `0xe5afa–0xe5b49` uses `0x2d9f30` on the original local region data. A value at most 0.3 chooses index `0xdb8` (3512), registered as `cloud02.cub` at `0x1deff4–0x1df059`. A higher value chooses `0xdb9` (3513), registered as `cloud-dark.cub` at `0x1df298–0x1df2fd`. For `0xdb8` the registration enables the full baked-color flag at model `+0x9a`, changes every nonblack model voxel to RGB bytes (100, 130, 180), and rebuilds the mesh. For `0xdb9` it sets model RGB additions at `+0xa0–0xa2` to (10, 0, 20) before loading, with the default half-color flag. Both use the cloud load mode with nonzero `+0x99`. Registration and fields are visible at `0x177ffa`, `0x1df08a–0x1df22c`, `0x1df293`, `0x16e6b0`, and `0x16f320`.

The model bake at `0x170700` emits exposed faces of occupied voxels and computes color for each of their four corners. In this load mode, black is empty and the generic primary-color exclusions are disabled. The face exposure helper `0x171db0` samples outward from the corner on the normal axis. For model radius 3, its offset is −1 for a negative normal or 0 for a positive normal. The two tangent axes use offsets −3 through +2. To get each sample weight, add 0.5 to each of the three integer offsets, divide the largest of their absolute values by 3, and subtract the quotient from one. Empty and out-of-bounds samples contribute to exposed weight. All samples contribute to total weight. The exposure is the larger of 0.5 and the exposed fraction when total weight is positive. Radius 3 comes from model `+0x9c` at `0x16e756`.

In `0x170530`, the exponent on that exposure is 1.2. The bake adds the model RGB additions to the voxel RGB in units of one byte over 255 and multiplies by the exponentiated exposure. It calculates luminance with (0.30, 0.59, 0.11) and rescales RGB by reciprocal luminance if luminance exceeds 1. Taking the resulting luminance as gray, it mixes gray toward the rescaled RGB with weight 3 minus twice the exponentiated exposure, then clamps the channels. The alpha before packing is 2. The flag at `+0x9a` leaves this RGBA full when set and halves each component when clear. The packer at `0xc3a10` truncates each component times 255 and stores the low byte alongside the byte-coded face normal and position. The active layout admits the packed color as normalized RGBA.

Cloud setup at `0xe57e3–0xe5821` clears point lights through `0xce580` and `0xc7010`, sets the white override and glow to zero, and sets material RGBA to (1, 1, 1, 1). The shared ambient and front/back directional light constants are supplied by the frame at `0xe3cc8` through `0xc6ce0` and `0xc6820`. With zero point-light radii, the vertex shader applies those constants using the model's transformed face normal. The ambient and back-light shape is half plus half the absolute dot with the fixed lighting direction. The front shape is the positive part of 0.7 times the dot with the supplied light direction plus 0.3. Packed vertex alpha weights the saturated front/back term before it adds to the ambient term. The shader doubles packed RGB, shapes its saturation using lit green and ambient alpha, and multiplies by the resulting normal-dependent lighting. The compiled vertex shader is the blob at `0x4387b0`; the cloud pixel shader uses its RGB and the separate draw-alpha buffer.

## Limits of the static trace

The cloud voxel payloads, the full semantics of the region and noise helpers, and the scene-specific range reached for a given loaded terrain set are unresolved here. The range values in the table describe the normal steady-state cap and are derived from the original writes and original float constants. Runtime appearance and alternate render paths are outside this static evidence.
