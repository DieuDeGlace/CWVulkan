# Original native event queues

This is a bounded original-native contract for the AMD64 PE32+ image cubeworld.original.exe.unpacked.exe, 5,757,952 bytes, preferred image base 0x140000000, SHA-256 7AF18CB28C8BF848F355287428C6390B2EA2A682CA466190F5EB9859C5D6F244. Addresses below are RVAs. Evidence is the original binary and its original function, assembly and call records. No game, probe or build was run. The words damage, sound, pickup and feat below describe independently observed native consumption; unidentified notices retain offset-based descriptions.

## Owners and common representation

The constructor at 0x7ea80 stores the supplied World pointer at Game+0x8 and constructs an event bundle inline at Game+0x1970. It also constructs a hosted object inline at Game+0x6e8 through 0xf17a0. That constructor constructs a World inline at hosted-object+0x8 through 0x29a130. The hosted World therefore has base Game+0x6f0 and its bundle is at Game+0x780. Independently, every World constructed by 0x29a130 has its bundle inline at World+0x90. These are owner paths; equality or aliasing with a separately supplied Game+0x8 pointer is not assumed.

The client object is inline at Game+0xd18. The call at 0x7eb46 supplies Game as its second argument to constructor 0x3aa10; that constructor stores it at client+0xa50. Packet receiver 0x3e450 obtains Game by this back pointer. Thus its received-event destination is Game+0x1970, not a bundle reached by treating the client itself as a World.

Initializer 0x3ac90 constructs sixteen adjacent headers in a 0x100-byte bundle. Each header occupies 0x10 bytes: an eight-byte pointer to an allocated sentinel at header+0x0 and an eight-byte element count at header+0x8. The sentinel is not inline in the header. Sentinel and ordinary nodes have an eight-byte next link at node+0x0 and previous link at node+0x8. An empty list has both sentinel links pointing to the sentinel and count zero. The payload of an ordinary node begins at node+0x10. The sentinel payload is not an event and is not initialized as one.

Construction and insertion at 0x36e00, 0x370f0, 0x36ff0, 0x36eb0 and the corresponding allocators establish value copies into separately allocated nodes. Insertion links a new node before the sentinel and increments the count. Header count and node links are separate ordinary memory writes. They are not an atomic publication protocol. Range-copy helpers such as 0x38e80/0x38120 and 0x39200/0x385f0 allocate destination nodes while preserving the source nodes. They do not splice source nodes into the destination.

## Bundle slots

All slot offsets in this section are relative to the bundle base. The matching World header is World+0x90 plus that offset; the matching received header is Game+0x1970 plus that offset. Sizes described as payload extents are the amounts serialized for a record, not evidence that every byte is an initialized semantic field.

Slot+0x00 is the damage list: payload extent 0x48, node allocation 0x58, allocator 0x3c2f0, parser 0x39970, writer 0xf0e70. Slot+0x10 is sound: extent 0x30, allocation 0x40, allocator 0x3c530, parser 0x3a250, writer 0xf0c90. Slot+0x20 is particle bursts: extent 0x48, allocation 0x58, allocator 0x3c470, parser 0x39fd0, writer 0xf0e70.

Slot+0x30 has a 0x1c serialized payload in a 0x30-byte node, allocator 0x3c290, parser 0x39840 and writer 0xf0de0. Its frame consumption concerns spatial block changes. Slot+0x40 has a 0x80-byte payload in a 0x90-byte node, allocator 0x3c1d0, parser 0x39690 and writer 0xf0d20. These two payload layouts are not expanded here. Slots+0x50 and +0x60 own nested, spatially keyed lists and are outside this simple-list contract.

Slot+0x70 has an eight-byte payload in a 0x18-byte node, allocator 0x3c5f0, parser 0x3a5a0 and writer 0xf1220. Its constructor 0x37200 copies two four-byte components. The frame uses them as the two coordinates of a zone lookup and clears that zone's byte at +0xf8. This is not evidence for an entity-ID notice at that slot.

Slot+0x80 is pickup: extent 0xd0, allocation 0xe0, allocator 0x3c3b0, parser 0x39af0, writer 0xf0f10. Slot+0x90 is a death-related pair: extent 0x10, allocation 0x20, allocator 0x3c410, parser 0x39ce0, writer 0xf1010. Slot+0xa0 is a feat notice: extent 0x28, allocation 0x38, allocator 0x3c4d0, parser 0x3a130, writer 0xf1090.

Slot+0xb0 is an actor relationship-value update: extent 0x18, allocation 0x28, allocator 0x3c170, parser 0x39470, writer 0xf0c00. Slot+0xc0 is an actor effect-record update: extent 0x30, allocation 0x40, allocator 0x9fe0, parser 0x39570, writer 0xf0c90. Slot+0xd0 carries one four-byte temporal notice in a 0x18-byte node, allocator 0x3c050, parser 0x39380, writer 0xf0b80. Slot+0xe0 carries an eight-byte conversation-trigger identifier in a 0x18-byte node, allocator 0xa1c0, parser 0x3a6a0, writer 0xf1220.

Slot+0xf0 is an owning wide-string list, allocated through 0x3c650 and specially cleared through 0x26a730. It must not be treated as a plain scalar list. Its string content and full behavior are outside this contract. The observed packet-7 serialization and parsing include slots through +0xe0 and omit +0xf0.

## Payload details

Offsets in this section are relative to the payload, so an ordinary node adds 0x10 to each offset. Constructors and serializers distinguish field widths from gaps; a raw transmitted byte in a gap is not thereby a defined field.

Damage copies eight-byte identifiers at +0x00 and +0x08, a four-byte value at +0x10, one byte at +0x14, four-byte values at +0x18 and +0x1c, three eight-byte components at +0x20, +0x28 and +0x30, three four-byte components at +0x38, +0x3c and +0x40, and individual bytes at +0x44, +0x45 and +0x46. Bytes +0x15 through +0x17 and +0x47 are not copied as fields by 0x38b80. Routine 0x29d980 prints the +0x10 value as damage from the first identifier to the second; 0x29dcf0 resolves the second identifier and can subtract that floating-point value from the resolved actor's health at +0x180. Other modes exist: frame 0xa5a50 branches on payload byte+0x45 and sometimes uses the quantity for mana or effect changes. It is not an unconditional health-delta record. The frame treats +0x18 as a signed four-byte quantity in one branch. This audit does not assign universal semantic names to the remaining scalars, vector components or flag bytes.

Sound has three signed eight-byte spatial components at +0x00, +0x08 and +0x10; a four-byte sound selector at +0x18; four-byte floating-point audio parameters at +0x1c and +0x20; and an eight-byte identifier at +0x28. Bytes +0x24 through +0x27 are not copied by constructor 0x370f0. At call site 0xaa58f, the frame supplies the selector, the spatial triple, +0x20 in the fourth floating-point argument position and +0x1c as the fifth argument to 0xa0420. That routine multiplies +0x20 into the volume calculation with distance attenuation and the Game volume setting. It forwards +0x1c to an audio virtual call; a particular API-level name such as pitch is not proved here. Hosted distribution 0xf33b0 uses +0x28 to suppress transmission to the matching peer identifier. The field therefore cannot be discarded as padding merely because the local audio call does not use it. Producers can consume RNG before appending sound; 0x2bb8e0 contains such a producer.

Particle bursts contain three eight-byte spatial components at +0x00, +0x08 and +0x10, followed by eleven four-byte fields at +0x18, +0x1c, +0x20, +0x24, +0x28, +0x2c, +0x30, +0x34, +0x38, +0x3c and +0x40. Constructors 0x36ff0 and 0x7fb50 copy exactly those fields. Bytes +0x44 through +0x47 are outside those field copies. Frame 0xa5a50 uses +0x18 through +0x20 as a three-float vector added to a randomized vector; it copies +0x24 through +0x30 as a four-component vector through 0x17170. It takes the positive signed four-byte +0x38 value as the number of particles to produce, +0x3c as a particle selector, and copies +0x34 into each produced particle. It consumes multiple rand calls per produced particle and appends a different, larger temporary particle representation to Game+0x2090. The four-component vector's complete rendering interpretation, the units of +0x34, and the meaning of +0x40 are not established by this bounded queue audit. These fields are not byte RGB values. The burst record must not be confused with the larger per-particle render/update record.

Pickup constructor 0x3ac10 copies an eight-byte actor identifier at +0x00; an inline item record beginning at +0x08 through 0x35400; an unsigned two-byte quantity at +0xa8; eight-byte spatial components at +0xb0, +0xb8 and +0xc0; and a four-byte flags value at +0xc8. The item occupies a 0xa0-byte area before the quantity. Its field-copy helper reaches item-relative byte+0x9d; this does not establish every intervening byte as meaningful. Pickup bytes +0xa6 through +0xa7, +0xaa through +0xaf and +0xcc through +0xcf are not fields copied by 0x3ac10. Frame consumption resolves the +0x00 identifier, transforms/copies the item through 0x10a720, and passes the two-byte quantity to inventory processing. For the local player, bit 1 of +0xc8 enables a branch using the three spatial components. Other flag meanings and the complete item subrecord are outside scope. The 0xd0-byte wire extent includes gaps and does not make their values stable.

The death-related payload at slot+0x90 consists of two eight-byte values copied by 0x36f00. Within the health-reaches-zero branch of 0x29dcf0, the second value is the dying actor's identifier; the first is zero when the actor's relationship map is empty, or a relationship-map key for each generated pair. Therefore the first value is not proved to identify one unique killer. The frame uses the second value in an operation on World+0x20 and later presents the pair to 0x2c3470. This audit does not prove a universal kill-credit rule or entity-destruction protocol from those uses.

The feat payload at slot+0xa0 contains an eight-byte recipient identifier at +0x00, individual bytes at +0x08 and +0x09, four-byte fields at +0x0c and +0x10, an eight-byte field at +0x18, and four-byte fields at +0x20 and +0x24. Constructor 0x37080 leaves +0x0a through +0x0b and +0x14 through +0x17 outside its field copies. Frame consumption checks the recipient against the local player, creates a visual notice and calls 0x935a0, whose result is the native wide text FEAT!. The remaining fields' detailed meanings are unproved here; this text does not establish a particular feat taxonomy.

The relationship update at slot+0xb0 copies three eight-byte chunks through 0x36ca0. Native producer 0x2c3200 places an actor identifier at +0x00, the affected actor identifier at +0x08, and a four-byte relationship-map value at +0x10. It can create a related value of 0.5. The frame resolves +0x08, selects its map at actor+0xb88 using +0x00, and writes the four-byte value at +0x10. Bytes +0x14 through +0x17 are copied/transmitted by the chunk-oriented record copy, but are not established as an additional value by this producer or consumer. Aggro is a plausible descriptive category for this behavior; no separate SDK layout or universal threat semantics are assumed.

The effect update at slot+0xc0 copies six eight-byte chunks through 0x7380. The frame resolves the actor identifier at +0x08 and passes the embedded record beginning at +0x10, together with byte+0x28, to 0x65390. The first identifier at +0x00 also participates in the local-player filtering condition. Routine 0x65390 appends or replaces a 0x18-byte record in actor+0x980, matching its first byte and its eight-byte component at embedded-record+0x10 in some modes. A complete effect taxonomy and meanings for all remaining bytes are not established.

The four-byte slot+0xd0 record has one bounded producer at 0x2e2e80: when the World time accumulator crosses its threshold, it increments the World counter at +0x08, invokes two World routines and, in one mode, appends the value zero to World+0x160 through 0x112f00. Client processing at 0x3f4f0 checks World+0x160 records for zero and triggers a connection-related operation. Calling this a sleep command with a proved duration would go beyond this evidence. Slot+0xe0 is an eight-byte identifier copied by 0x75c0; the frame resolves it as an actor and invokes 0x2d9480 to generate conversation output. The triggering operation can therefore have effects beyond list traversal. Its entire transitive scope is not specified here.

## Receiving, consuming and resetting

Packet receiver 0x3e450 accepts packet type 7 on its observed peer-matching route. It reads a four-byte compressed-buffer length, fills a temporary buffer, calls 0x66d10, constructs a temporary bundle, parses records into it and range-copies them into the corresponding Game received headers. It then destroys the temporary bundle through 0x3b420 and the byte buffer through 0x33770. The received scalar records remain as independently allocated destination nodes; pointers into the temporary nodes do not survive. Record counts on this wire are four-byte quantities, although in-memory counts are eight bytes. Simple serializers 0xf0b80 through 0xf1220 traverse without removing their source nodes. They transmit fixed payload extents and preserve some raw gap bytes. Several parsers clamp an exhausted cursor without establishing a valid fully initialized record, so malformed or truncated packet input is outside the valid-layout guarantee.

Frame 0xa5a50 first processes received records and copies selected lists into the World reached through Game+0x08. The copied groups include sound, bursts, pickups, death-related pairs, feats, temporal notices and conversation triggers. Relationship and effect updates receive their own earlier application logic; damage is processed separately and selected records are appended to World+0x90. An unconditional one-for-one replay of all received lists is not established. The frame then replaces the received bundle with an empty bundle through 0x83640 and destroys the displaced storage through 0x3b420. Later in that same frame, 0x3f4f0 can deliver further packet-7 records, after the earlier received-bundle reset; such records are not retroactively part of the earlier consumption pass.

The frame consumes World sound, bursts, pickups and notices through ordinary list walks, and near its end replaces World+0x90 with another empty bundle through 0x83640. The helper at 0x83ba0 used in some walks advances an iterator; it is not an individual node eraser. Empty replacement uses 0x82cd0 for the simple lists: it frees destination elements and exchanges sentinel pointer and count with the empty source. Consequently the sentinel address itself may change, as well as every event-node address becoming invalid. Destruction at 0x3b420 frees simple nodes and sentinels; 0xcc140 is a direct simple-list destructor. Neither the count nor the sentinel pointer is a durable generation identifier.

Hosted worker 0x83e60 calls 0xf5520 with Game+0x6e8. Its send path snapshots a per-peer bundle through 0xf1b50, replaces that peer bundle with empty storage through 0x83640, serializes packet type 7 from the snapshot, and destroys the snapshot. Later it simulates the embedded World, distributes World+0x90 through 0xf33b0 into per-peer bundles, then resets that World bundle. Assembly at 0xf69aa supplies hosted-object+0x98, exactly embedded-World+0x90, as the distribution source. Distribution copies and filters records; sound excludes a matching peer identifier and sound/burst distribution includes spatial filtering. Thus the hosted World bundle and a particular peer's outgoing bundle need not contain identical records. The per-peer tree and all its node/key invariants are outside scope.

## Established protection and its limits

World constructor 0x29a130 initializes inline critical sections at +0x3c8, +0x3f0 and +0x418. Helpers 0x2abb70 and 0x2b9890 enter and leave World+0x3c8. Helpers 0x2abb80 and 0x2b98a0 enter and leave World+0x3f0. These are addresses of inline critical sections, not pointer fields containing lock objects.

On the observed frame path, 0xa5a50 enters the World+0x3c8 section before received-list application and retains it through the event consumers, the later client drain and final World-bundle reset. The native call sites are 0xa6464 for entry and 0xadb23 for release. Client drain 0x3f4f0 briefly enters client+0xa18 to copy and clear its raw-message list at client+0xa40. It releases that section before invoking packet receiver 0x3e450. The observed nesting is therefore frame World+0x3c8 enclosing the raw-message handoff section, followed by packet decoding while still within the frame's World section. Client+0xa18 alone is not established as protecting Game+0x1970 during packet decoding or frame consumption.

On the hosted worker path, 0xf5520 enters hosted-object+0x5f8 at its beginning and retains it through outgoing snapshot work, simulation, event distribution and final reset. Earlier it briefly nests hosted-object+0x5d0, releasing that before the later World-section acquisition. Later it enters the embedded World+0x3c8 through 0x2abb70, distributes events, clears the embedded World bundle, leaves that World section and finally leaves hosted-object+0x5f8. Thus hosted-object+0x5f8 enclosing embedded-World+0x3c8 is an established native nesting on this route. The earlier serialization of per-peer snapshots is enclosed by hosted-object+0x5f8, rather than by the later embedded-World section.

The simple insertion, parsing, copying, serialization and clearing routines examined here do not themselves acquire these critical sections. These facts bound what their observed callers hold; they do not prove that one lock universally excludes every writer, that all owner lifetimes are protected by it, or that holding the section produces a coherent snapshot across the supplied World, the hosted World and the client. This audit does not supply a complete writer census, shutdown protocol, exception-unwind lock proof, transitive callback/lock census, or a universal order involving World+0x3f0. A bounded read must be understood against the particular live owner and observed caller scope. No engine synchronization or capture policy follows from this note.
