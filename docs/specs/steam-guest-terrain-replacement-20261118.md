# Steam guest terrain replacement and readback

Evidence is the original x64 Steam image `cubeworld.original.exe.unpacked.exe`, SHA-256 `7AF18CB28C8BF848F355287428C6390B2EA2A682CA466190F5EB9859C5D6F244`, and its original instruction and routine exports. Addresses are RVAs relative to `0x140000000`.

## Which records reach the guest

The host appends current terrain changes to the peer's outgoing list at `0xF343E`. When servicing that peer's zone requests, it also appends the requested resident zone's nonempty edit history to that same list, in forward order (`0xF3843` through `0xF3865`, `0x38070`). Both sources therefore go through the same guest dispatch. The type-7 world update carries a four-byte count and 28 bytes per change. In each record the absolute signed block X, Y and Z occupy offsets 0, 4 and 8. Six old bytes start at 12, six new bytes at 18, and the four-byte day at 24. Integer fields are little-endian. The reader queues complete records in wire order (`0xF0DE0`, `0x3E450`, `0x39840`, `0x38E00`). A six-byte block contains RGB in bytes 0–2, another byte at 3, material at 4 and flags at 5. The following rules apply to byte values directly.

## Applying a network change

The guest visits the incoming records first to last. At `0xA80C0` it inspects only the *new* material and flags to choose the physical operation. It tests neither the old value against the terrain nor the day against the current day.

- **Both material and flags are zero.** The guest calls the removal operation at `0xA80DB`. It ignores the incoming RGB and byte 3. If the resident column already contains this Z, the six stored bytes become `FF FF FF 00 00 00` above Z zero or `FF FF FF 00 02 00` at Z zero and below. Outside the existing vertical span, removal leaves the column alone. A missing zone also has no physical write (`0x91160`, `0x935E0`, initializers `0x1CE0` and `0x1D20`).
- **Either material or flags is nonzero.** The guest passes the complete new six-byte value through the world setter at `0xA80FA`. It looks up the resident zone and calls the general zone setter `0x31C1C0`. Above Z zero the setter stores all six supplied bytes exactly. At Z zero and below, material zero with flag bit `0x02` clear is replaced in full by `FF FF FF 00 02 00`, even if other flag bits are set. A nonzero material or flags containing `0x02` preserves all six supplied bytes at any elevation. The test and replacement are at `0x31C2BE` through `0x31C2E1`; the low-elevation constant is initialized at `0x5F20`.

The guest separately looks up the resident zone using signed floor division of X and Y by 64. When that lookup succeeds, it appends the original 28-byte record to the resident history even if a removal missed the vertical span. The append preserves the transmitted old bytes, new bytes, day, order and duplicates (`0xA816A` through `0xA8184`, `0xA1680`, `0x38B00`). The physical replacement is never substituted into the copied record. For an unloaded zone, neither world setter creates a zone, and the resident-history append is skipped. Later, the guest also copies the received records into its world update collection (`0xA8775`). This is distinct from applying or storing an edit in a missing zone. The traced guest pass supplies no deferred per-zone write on a miss. A subsequent requested-zone history from the host can carry the edit again.

## Bounds, gaps and terrain queries

The general setter rejects an X/Y outside its zone's 64-by-64 footprint. A column stores a base Z and a consecutive run of six-byte blocks. Its upper bound is the base plus the count, exclusive. A proposed value with material and flags both zero is ignored at or above that upper bound. The same value below the base can extend the column *when sent directly to the general setter*. This distinction matters for local replay and undo. Network removal does not extend in either direction. For a zero-length column the upper-bound test is performed against its current base before the first accepted write sets the base to the target Z.

A nonzero material or nonzero flags permits expansion on either side. Raising the top preserves old entries and initializes intervening new slots to six zero bytes. Lowering the base preserves old entries at their old world coordinates. Between the new target and the former base, exclusive, the general setter fills missing levels from the terrain generator. For those fill blocks it halves the generated RGB values, clamps to the range 0–255 and discards the fractional part. The other three generated bytes are copied. The target itself gets the selected replacement. An accepted write into an empty column creates its first entry directly at the target (`0x31C1C0`, `0xA36E0`, `0x6D030`, `0x6CB30`, `0x6CAF0`).

For a world block query (`0xE9A0`, `0xEB90`, `0xE770`), an unloaded zone or a Z below a resident column's base returns `C8 C8 C8 00 01 00`. At or above the column's upper bound it returns `FF FF FF 00 00 00` for positive Z and `FF FF FF 00 02 00` for nonpositive Z. In range it reads all six stored bytes. For nonpositive Z, a stored zero material with flag bit `0x02` clear reads as `FF FF FF 00 02 00`. This also affects the zero-filled expansion gaps. In-range zero-filled gaps at positive Z read as `00 00 00 00 00 00`. Above the top, the positive-Z default has white RGB instead. Initializers are `0x10E0`, `0x1100` and `0x1120`.

## Local saved-zone replay and resident undo

Local saved-zone replay differs from receiving a host's requested-zone history. In `0x2AE0C0`, the branch for a newly generated local zone copies the matching saved history and replays each record's six new bytes directly through `0x31C1C0`, in forward order (`0x2AE2B1`, `0x2AE2D8`). The guest-mode branch skips that local saved-state lookup. For local replay, even zero material and zero flags reach the general setter, so custom RGB and byte 3 can survive at an existing positive Z. The same six bytes arriving over the network take the removal path.

The session transition at `0x3D820` invokes resident undo at `0x2DA090`. For each resident zone with history, undo visits the records from last to first and sends the six **old** bytes directly to `0x31C1C0` at their recorded absolute positions. It then clears the resident list, marks the zone for refresh and records a refresh timestamp. The general setter's material, flags, bounds and elevation rules still apply. Duplicate edits are undone in reverse order. A missing zone has no resident history to traverse. Neither the saved-zone replay nor the undo compares the stored day before writing.

## Examples and visual scope

- At an existing Z of 6, a received new block `10 20 30 40 00 00` physically becomes `FF FF FF 00 00 00`. The history keeps `10 20 30 40 00 00`. Given that same six-byte value on local saved-zone replay, the existing slot reads `10 20 30 40 00 00`.
- At Z zero or -2, `10 20 30 40 00 10` physically becomes `FF FF FF 00 02 00`. At those same coordinates, `10 20 30 40 00 12` and `10 20 30 40 01 10` each retain all six original bytes. At positive Z, `10 20 30 40 00 10` also retains all six. These examples assume a resident zone.
- For a resident column covering Z 3 through 5, an incoming removal at Z 9 leaves the column unchanged and still appends its raw history record. An incoming material-1 block at Z 9 expands the column. Z 6 through 8 then read as six zero bytes, and Z 9 reads back the supplied material-1 block. For block X -1 and Y 64, the required zone key is (-1, 1). An absent zone yields the gray material-1 query fallback, independently of the received color.

The physical results above describe column storage and the ordinary simulation query. The terrain geometry path at `0xE9530` also samples neighboring cells through `0xDA660` and `0xEB90`, and branches on neighbor materials including material 2. A stored RGB value alone therefore does not determine the color of a visible pixel or whether the renderer emits a face. The source evidence here does not establish frame timing or rendered appearance for a particular guest session.
