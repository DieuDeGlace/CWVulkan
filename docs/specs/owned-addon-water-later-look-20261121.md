# User-authored water appearance

FINAL / STABLE / HELD. This report describes the water add-on additions in commits ea53e72 and f43b42f. Its evidence consists of the added water pixel-shader lines in both revisions and the identifiable added water-color and fog declarations in the later renderer revision.

## Earlier and later color

The earlier revision, ea53e72, samples the refracted opaque scene at texture level zero. For each color channel, transmission weights the scene sample and the complementary weight supplies the water body color. It also adds a comparison between the surface normal and the direction to the camera against zero. The additions do not show the action governed by that comparison, the earlier absorption coefficients, the source of its body color or the opacity it writes.

The later revision, f43b42f, retains that per-channel color mixture and specifies its inputs. Above water, the body color in RGB order is (0.00, 0.33, 0.76) multiplied by brightness. Underwater, it uses the supplied fog color. The renderer additions name a maximum underwater fog distance of 48 blocks and give a blue palette. The two sky colors are (0.02, 0.45, 0.82) and (0.00, 0.35, 0.72). The fog color is (0.00, 0.42, 0.79). Each RGB triplet is multiplied by brightness and has alpha one. The permitted additions leave the brightness calculation, the enclosing renderer branch, the effective range when the current view distance is shorter than 48, and the final surface alpha unresolved.

## Wave basis and distance

The later shader classifies an incoming face as a top surface when its normal's vertical component is greater than 0.5. For such a face it derives the normal used in the following optics from the fragment's supplied horizontal world position. Its first phase projects that position onto (0.65, 0.31) and its second onto (-0.27, 0.72). Each projection receives the corresponding supplied phase offset. The shader comment describes these offsets as containing a world-position contribution and a speed-times-time contribution, wrapped to one turn in radians. The permitted changes omit the code supplying them. The absolute reference position, phase speeds, signs of temporal travel and time units therefore remain unresolved.

Five traveling components use integer multiples of these two phases. In order, their pairs of phase multipliers and nominal heights in blocks are (1, 0) at 0.12, (0, 1) at 0.11, (2, 1) at 0.05, (1, -2) at 0.05, and (3, 2) at 0.025. For each component, the matching combination of the two horizontal direction vectors gives its spatial frequency. The component contributes that vector scaled by its height and the cosine of its combined phase. Integer multipliers preserve continuity when a supplied phase wraps.

The negative sum of these slopes tilts an upward normal, which the shader normalizes. The slope keeps full strength through 40 blocks of camera distance, fades smoothly from 40 to 120, and is zero at and beyond 120. Camera distance is the length of the full direction-to-camera vector. These additions establish a per-pixel change in the lighting and refraction normal. They provide no measurement of mesh displacement.

## Refraction, depth and reflection

The later revision offsets the screen sampling coordinates by 0.006 times the horizontal components of the normal. It clamps the result half a pixel inside each texture edge. It takes the difference between scene and surface depth, clamps the thickness to 0.3 through 100, and computes each channel's transmission by exponential attenuation. The coefficients, in RGB order, are 0.60, 0.45 and 1.00 per block. At one block about 54.9 percent of the red, 63.8 percent of the green and 36.8 percent of the blue background sample remains. Thus the blue background channel actually attenuates fastest. The bright blue seen at depth comes from the body-color contribution as the background fades. The depth reconstruction, the choice of coordinates used to read scene depth, and any extra handling at foreground edges are outside the added evidence.

Only an above-water top surface enters the added sun-reflection calculation. The shader reflects the normalized incoming view direction about the perturbed surface normal and measures alignment with the supplied direction toward the sun. It clamps the alignment to zero through one, raises it to power 300, multiplies by 3, and clamps again. Brightness scales that result. A second multiplier clamps five times the sun direction's vertical component to zero through one. It extinguishes this glint at or below the horizon and reaches full strength at a vertical component of 0.2. If the resulting glint exceeds 0.001, the shader multiplies it by the solar visibility obtained at the fragment's world position with a vertical upward normal, then adds the result to the composited color. The wavy normal steers the glint while the visibility query receives the fixed upward normal. The shadow sampling method and any additional sky, environment, Fresnel or scene reflections remain unestablished by these additions.

The underwater body color, 48-block fog ceiling and exclusion from the added sun glint are separately evidenced. The submerged-state test itself, feature selection, final opacity or blend state, and the full difference between the two revisions cannot be reconstructed from zero-context additions alone. In particular, exponential transmission specifies the background's contribution to RGB, independently of the unknown output alpha.
