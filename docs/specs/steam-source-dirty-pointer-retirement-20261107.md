# Steam source dirty-pointer retirement

This bounded original-Steam contract traces the source-pointer memberships at World+0x190 and World+0x1A0 through the inspected physical retirement, source destruction, installation and viewed-worker exit paths. It also establishes one ordinary host final-capture versus embedded-World teardown order. The supported original x64 image is binary/cubeworld.original.exe.unpacked.exe under C:/Users/DieuDeGlace/Documents/decompiled cube world steam, base 0x140000000, timestamp 0x5D93AB0D, SHA256 7AF18CB28C8BF848F355287428C6390B2EA2A682CA466190F5EB9859C5D6F244. The image hash was checked for this audit. All addresses below are original image RVAs.

Reference filenames were selected before scoped content inspection. Principal originals are decompiled/functions/1402d2410.c, 1402d2670.c, 1402d2930.c, 1402d3e20.c, 1402ae0c0.c, 140299640.c, 1402abd20.c, 14029c840.c, 140299d40.c, 1402f9400.c, 1402f9940.c, 1402fa3b0.c, 1400842d0.c, 140084f70.c, 1402e30b0.c, 14029b290.c, 14029c140.c, 1402ac600.c, 14008aad0.c and 140087a90.c. Host-owner evidence is 1400f17a0.c, 1400f1f20.c, 1400f2f70.c, 1400f2340.c, 1400f3220.c, 1400f1c90.c, 1402d38e0.c, 14009af20.c, 14007d560.c, 1400f0150.c and 14003c7d0.c, with bounded retained-map copy checks in 14006d400.c, 14006f160.c, 14006f800.c and 14006e2e0.c. Original decompiled/assembly.txt and the image's concrete vtable entries resolve the relevant deleting and thread-entry routes. No replacement material is part of this evidence.

The two dirty collections retain eight-byte source addresses. They are separate from the coordinate-keyed loaded-source map at World+0x460 and from the retained-value map at World+0x10. Routine 0x2E30B0 proves their direct publication use: it walks World+0x190, immediately writes source byte+0xF9 and reads source coordinates and the source+0x30 array; it then walks World+0x1A0, immediately writes source byte+0xFA and reads coordinates and the source+0x60 array. It appends the copied groups to outgoing World+0xE0 and World+0xF0 respectively. These reached reads use the stored source address without first looking it up again by coordinate in World+0x460.

After its reached publication work, 0x2E30B0 frees both collections' tree nodes, restores their empty headers and zeros the 64-bit counts at World+0x198 and World+0x1A8. The first clear's native sequence begins at 0x2E3443; the second ends with count zeroing at 0x2E34D5. That operation clears pointer membership, not the source objects. It is a publication routine with source dereferences before its clear, rather than a standalone safe discard of arbitrary stored addresses.

The concrete source constructor 0x2F9400 stores its owning World pointer at source+0x8 and installs vtable 0x4738C0. The original image's first entry in that vtable is 0x2FA3B0. That deleting wrapper calls the source destructor 0x2F9940 at 0x2FA3BF and, when its low deletion flag is set, frees the source allocation afterward. The ordinary removers described below pass deletion flag 1. The destructor releases source-owned model, mesh, array, map and block-column storage. Its directly inspected body does not use source+0x8 to remove the address from the owning World's dirty collections, and the deleting wrapper adds no such operation.

The inspected coordinate-map node eraser at 0x2ABD20 only updates that map's bucket/list links and count, frees its map node and returns the following iterator. It does not destroy the mapped source or reconcile World+0x190/+0x1A0. Thus removing a loaded-map entry and destroying its source are explicit separate operations on these paths. Neither one supplies a directly proved per-source dirty-membership erasure. The same numeric offsets inside source-owned storage must not be mistaken for accesses to those fields on the owning World.

The viewed single-source retirement routine 0x2D2410 selects a nonnull loaded source, then holds World+0x3F0 and World+0x3C8 while recording retained state and unlinking its coordinate-map entry. It calls 0x2D3E20 at 0x2D25FA before the map-node eraser at 0x2D260E. It releases both guards before invoking the source's deleting virtual entry at 0x2D2654. The direct routine contains no dirty-set erase or clear. Consequently its selected source's loaded-map membership ends before its allocation is destroyed, while no corresponding end of an existing dirty-pointer membership is established by these direct operations.

The ordinary host required-set retirement routine 0x2D2670 has a different recording order. After its selection pass, each selected source is processed under World+0x3F0 followed by World+0x3C8. It erases the coordinate-map node at 0x2D2892, then calls 0x2D3E20 at 0x2D289D while retaining the source pointer locally. Both guards are released before the deleting virtual call at 0x2D28CC. Again, no World+0x190/+0x1A0 reconciliation is present in the direct remover, map eraser or concrete source deleting wrapper.

Retained-value recording is also distinct from dirty membership. The inspected 0x2D3E20 body records the selected source+0x30 array when source byte+0xF9 is nonzero and the source+0x60 array when source byte+0xFA is nonzero, setting the corresponding retained-record override flags. Its other reached edit/summary/scalar recording is outside the array-membership question. This helper does not consume either dirty-pointer collection or make pending membership alone equivalent to either source byte. The inspected retirement paths do not first invoke 0x2E30B0 to publish pending memberships.

On the inspected viewed render/preparation-worker exit route, 0x842D0 calls 0x2D2930 on the World reached through Game+0x8 at 0x84F2F. This is the exit path reached from the Game byte+0x2068 stop test and another directly reached jump to that same exit block. The separate source-generation worker 0x84F70 has its own exit body and is not substituted for this concrete bulk-teardown call.

Routine 0x2D2930 takes a temporary snapshot of the coordinate/source-pointer map through 0x29C840 under World+0x3F0. It empties the actual loaded-source map, frees its original nodes and resets its buckets before releasing that guard at 0x2D29E7. It then walks the temporary entries. For each nonnull source pointer it enters World+0x3C8, records retained state through 0x2D3E20 at 0x2D2A1B, leaves that guard, and invokes the source deleting entry at 0x2D2A3E. It finally destroys the temporary map storage. The temporary coordinate entries carry source addresses; this does not extend their allocation lifetime beyond the explicit deleting call.

This bulk viewed-worker teardown empties World+0x460, not World+0x190 or World+0x1A0. Its inspected direct body and directly resolved source destruction do not clear those two dirty sets. If one contained a retiring source address and no separate writer removed it, this path itself leaves that stored address unreconciled with the ended source lifetime. This is a conditional lifetime finding: it does not establish that a later publication call actually visits freed storage, or that every execution has outstanding memberships when the worker exits.

Installation at an existing coordinate needs a separate distinction. The inspected source factory 0x2AE0C0 first looks up World+0x460. At 0x2AE114 through 0x2AE129, a present nonnull mapped source is returned unchanged. An ordinary request for an already loaded coordinate through this route therefore does not itself replace or destroy that source. Missing entries and entries with null mapped values proceed to fresh source construction/generation and the admitted retained-value restoration.

At the final installation boundary, 0x2AE0C0 enters World+0x3F0 and then World+0x3C8, obtains or inserts the coordinate entry through 0x299640 at 0x2AE34A, and writes the new source address into its mapped value at 0x2AE354. This late store neither reads and destroys a displaced source address nor transfers, erases or retargets dirty-set membership. The coordinate helper returns an existing entry when found. The ordinary initial nonnull-hit case has already returned, so this audit does not invent a normal single-threaded nonnull-replacement case or prove which alternate/concurrent change could make the late store overwrite one.

Physical removal followed by a later fresh installation at the same coordinate does not make old dirty entries coordinate references. Those entries still contain addresses. The inspected final store supplies no conversion from an earlier source object's membership to the newly installed object. If an address is reused by the allocator, raw address equality alone supplies no proved object-generation identity; actual reuse and additional writers remain unresolved. No broader source-replacement entrypoint census is claimed.

A full World-object destruction provides a separate, proved cleanup boundary. Its concrete deleting entry is 0x29C140, resolved from original World vtable 0x472040, and it reaches destructor 0x29B290. After its earlier live-resident destruction, that destructor visits the loaded-source map. For each nonnull source it records retained state at 0x29B303 and then invokes the source deleting entry at 0x29B31A. These source recording/deletion calls precede destruction of the World's guards and the later dirty-set cleanup.

Only later in 0x29B290 does the destructor clear World+0x1A0 at 0x29BA15 and World+0x190 at 0x29BA4A, then free their sentinels. The full-range helper 0x2AC600 reaches 0x8AAD0; its recursive helper 0x87A90 frees tree nodes without dereferencing or destroying their source-address payloads. It resets the headers and counts. This is a discard of remaining memberships after source destruction, not a publication of the queued source arrays and not a per-source erasure performed by 0x2F9940.

The ordinary host final-capture order can be proved for the Host's own embedded World. Constructor 0xF17A0 constructs that World at Host+0x8 and creates a thread whose record carries the Host pointer. The inspected start route is 0xF0150 through 0x3C7D0 and the original thread-record vtable at 0x43FD18. Its entry resolves to thunk 0xF3070, which reaches 0xF3220; that routine invokes the host source-loader loop 0xF2340 at 0xF3292. The resulting thread handle/identifier is stored at Host+0x558.

Host destructor 0xF1F20 writes zero to Host byte+0x550 at 0xF1F45 and joins that thread through 0x9AF20 at 0xF1F78. The inspected join helper calls the native thread-join entry, checks success, and clears the stored handle/identifier. On the reached successful ordinary path, the host destructor continues its own cleanup and finally reaches its embedded World destructor by the tail call at 0xF2334. Exceptional joins or aborted teardown are outside this ordering claim.

The host loader's ordinary exit body in 0xF2340 performs its final capture before returning to that successful join. It enters the embedded World's World+0x3C8 guard, copies the state record beginning at Host+0x10 through 0xF1C90 at 0xF2EFF, leaves the guard at 0xF2F0D, and passes the captured value to 0x2D38E0 at 0xF2F1E. The capture contains day/time and the reached retained-value collections, including the retained source-state map at World+0x10. Its inspected copy route constructs a separate destination tree and copies entries, rather than walking the currently loaded-source map and calling 0x2D3E20 on every loaded source.

Routine 0x2D38E0 admits its serialization/submission route only when World+0x210 is nonnull. The reached route serializes the supplied captured value and submits it under the state key through World+0x3B8. The loader then destroys its temporary capture and returns. This establishes the final in-memory capture and submission call boundary; codec details, later backend behavior and actual disk completion are not established here.

For this same embedded Host World and successful destructor/join chain, the final loader capture at 0xF2EFF and its reached submission therefore occur before the World destructor's recording of still-loaded sources at 0x29B303. The latter recording is not a prerequisite operation inside that final capture body. It cannot be assumed to contribute newly changed values to a value already captured earlier. This does not establish that those source values had never been recorded by an earlier retirement or periodic route, that the captured values necessarily differ, or what any later writer/backend operation ultimately persists.

That host ordering is not inferred from the viewed worker's 0x84F2F teardown. The viewed route follows Game+0x8, whereas the proved host sequence uses the World embedded at the same Host+0x8 throughout its thread binding, join and destruction. This audit does not establish identity or cross-thread order between arbitrary Worlds reached through those two access chains. Its local guard scopes and one successful host join do not prove universal quiescence, pointer safety or ordering for unrelated owners.

The directly proved dirty-membership cleanup boundaries in this scope are the reached 0x2E30B0 publication clear and the later full-World destructor clear. No per-source dirty-pointer erasure is established by the inspected physical removers, concrete source destructor, deleting wrapper, loaded-map installation store or viewed-worker bulk teardown. Indirect/global stores, alternate replacement/removal callers, aliased references and complete concurrency behavior remain explicit limits; absence of such writers is not claimed. No replacement implementation, history, tests, owner outputs/captures, reconstruction, SDK/dependency source or implementation result was used. Every earlier held contract remains unchanged.
