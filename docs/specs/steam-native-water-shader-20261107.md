# Native Steam water shader: bounded original-evidence contract

FINAL / STABLE / HELD. Supported image: original Steam x64 executable, preferred base 0x140000000, PE timestamp 0x5D93AB0D, SHA256 7AF18CB28C8BF848F355287428C6390B2EA2A682CA466190F5EB9859C5D6F244. Shader identity was resolved through original creation RVA 0xC60D0, selector RVA 0xC3DC0, bind RVA 0xC5FF0, and original-image bytes. The vertex stage is shader model 4.0 at image RVA 0x4387B0, creation length and DXBC header length both 0x1860 bytes, SHA256 E20DF6E0AE5C44FAC0AE530A357DE598C9829E5FA151599EEE6B97DE6114A56B. The water pixel stage is shader model 4.0 at RVA 0x43B790, both lengths 0xEF4 bytes, SHA256 D26EBB64D66F11240B3510A8443418A1226C115B971508C9FB50B91CF6B589F6. These are independently resolved identities. They are not inferred from an older address label. Creation places these objects at shader-object offsets 8 and 0x28; mode 9 and RVA 0xC5FF0 bind precisely those fields. The vertex stage is shared with the ordinary packed-geometry binding at RVA 0xC5DE0, which also binds shader-object offset 8. Water does not select a distinct water vertex program in this path.

The ordinary water draw at RVA 0xE85E0 is called by frame RVA 0xE2E00. It traverses the selected chunk list and each chunk's water-section list in two sweeps. Mode 5 is selected first; it uses the shared vertex stage and the pixel object at shader offset 0x10, created from original RVA 0x43A010 with length 0xE88. This first sweep uses the color-write-mask-zero blend state. Mode 9 is then selected for the water pixel stage and full RGBA writes. Both select the same depth-enabled, depth-writing state with comparison value 4 and the no-culling rasterizer state. The mode-9 color blend uses source value 5, destination value 6, and addition value 1, corresponding to source-alpha and inverse-source-alpha color blending. Its alpha blend uses source value 2, destination value 1, and addition value 1. These state-object selections are established by RVA 0xC3DC0 and creation RVA 0xC3150. The numeric pixel references below stop before blending and target-format conversion.

Before the first sweep, the pass sets vertex glow to zero, depth offset to zero, material RGBA from renderer offset 0x530, and zero point-light arrays through RVA 0xCE580. It loads the pixel alpha multiplier as a 32-bit float from renderer offset 0x4A8; assembly at RVA 0xE8663 establishes a float load, despite the decompiler's misleading integer presentation. The second sweep sets the view and projection matrices from renderer offsets 0x39C and 0x3DC and supplies each chunk's point-light position/radius array at chunk offset 0x240 and color array at chunk offset 0x340. The point-light upload at RVA 0xC7010 packages ten position/radius vectors followed by ten color vectors into the 0x140-byte vertex constant buffer. The CPU cache compares larger sixteen-entry arrays, but this shader consumes only the ten uploaded entries. On leaving the pass, the alpha setter is called with 1. The pass does not establish that renderer offset 0x4A8 has no other writers.

The frame initially sets renderer material offset 0x530 to RGBA 1,1,1,1. If its queried current cell has native type 2, it changes that material to 0.5,0.5,1,1. Per-section world matrices in the water pass have identity linear parts and a translation: horizontal chunk coordinates are scaled by 64 and combined with the renderer's fixed-point origin offsets, converted with 1/65536; the vertical translation is the section base at offset 0x5C. Thus the water normal and directional-light transforms have no section rotation or scale in this ordinary path. Broader world-origin construction and environment selection are outside this contract.

Vertex constant-buffer slots are: 0 projection matrix, 1 view matrix, 2 world matrix, 3 material RGBA, 4 the ten point positions/radii and ten point colors, 5 front-light RGBA, back-light RGBA, and directional-light vector, 6 ambient RGBA, 7 whitening scalar, 8 clip-depth offset, and 9 glow scalar. Binding RVA 0xC68C0 establishes these slots. Matrix setters at RVAs 0xC7240, 0xC78B0, and 0xC7AB0 transpose the native stored matrices for upload. Directional-light setter RVA 0xC6CE0 normalizes its supplied direction, uploads the two directional colors and direction, and forwards ambient RGBA to RVA 0xC6820. Immediately before water, the frame supplies front and back colors from renderer offsets 0x50C and 0x51C, its current directional vector, and ambient from offset 0x4FC. Whitening is a retained input at this boundary; the water routine does not itself set it. Its behavior is specified below without assuming an unproved retained value.

The packed input layout created at RVA 0xC60D0 consists of two four-component unsigned normalized byte attributes in one eight-byte vertex: POSITION at byte 0 and COLOR at byte 4. Packer RVA 0xC3A10 writes the first three bytes as local integer coordinates, the fourth as an axis-normal ordinal, and the final four as RGB plus a scalar multiplied by 255 and truncated to integer bytes. The shared vertex stage restores POSITION XYZ to voxel units by multiplying the normalized values by 255 and uses homogeneous position 1. Normal ordinal 0 selects positive X, 1 negative X, 2 positive Y, 3 negative Y, 4 positive Z, and all remaining values select negative Z. The ordinal comparisons operate on normalized byte values; the exact compiled values for ordinals 1 through 4 have bits 0x3B808081, 0x3C008081, 0x3C40C0C1, and 0x3C808081.


The water coordinate contract in mesher RVA 0xE9530 is relative to a zone and a vertical mesh group. The two zone indices are signed 32-bit inputs whose horizontal origins are their respective values multiplied by 64. Ordinary water-face vertices subtract those horizontal origins before packing, producing local X and Y corner coordinates from 0 through 64. Vertical groups are 250 units apart relative to the mesher's computed minimum height, not necessarily multiples of 250 relative to absolute zero. A group's base is that minimum plus 250 for each preceding group. Ordinary cell Z selects its group using its nonnegative height above the computed minimum divided by 250; the cell's local Z subtracts that group's base and face corners may add 1. The group object stores its signed 32-bit base at offset 0x5C, increasing that base by 250 for the next group. The ordinary corner construction fits local Z into 0 through 250. The packed X, Y, and Z fields are each independent unsigned eight-bit components; byte 3 is the independent normal ordinal, not additional coordinate bits. The packer itself writes low bytes and does not supply a general coordinate-range check. The shader's normalized-byte-times-255 reconstruction does not sign-extend local coordinates.

When the mesher's whole-zone sea optimization is admitted, it selects the vertical group containing absolute Z 0 and emits a four-corner quad spanning local horizontal corners 0,0; 64,0; 64,64; and 0,64. Its local Z is 1 minus that selected group's base. The pass's later WORLD vertical translation by the same stored base therefore places the quad at absolute height 1 before the view transform, rather than treating its packed Z as an already absolute height. Ordinary water faces and this sea quad use the same zone-horizontal and group-vertical anchors. The sea quad is appended to that group's shared packed vertex vector, with its six indices added to the same water-index vector; direct index helper RVA 0xCDEA0 and ordinary-face helper RVA 0xCD7D0 were inspected only to establish that shared group association. The sea quad uses the positive-Z ordinal and directly packs constant RGBA 0.25,0.5,0.5,1, producing color bytes 63,127,127,255 through truncation. It therefore does not inherit the ordinary face's sampled occlusion and illumination calculation. The inspected native optimization also suppresses the corresponding ordinary type-2 cell at absolute Z 0 after admitting the zone quad.

In the draw, WORLD adds the zone's horizontal origin together with renderer offsets 0x428 and 0x430 converted from fixed-point units by 1/65536, and adds the group's stored base to local Z. The frame sets those two renderer offsets to the negatives of its supplied fixed-point horizontal origin. These anchor additions apply to the same group geometry regardless of whether a water face or sea-quad triangle supplied its indices. The mesh builder's group list at chunk offset 0x1D8 and the draw's active list at chunk offset 0x1C8 are distinct; the intervening publication/swap mechanism was not audited here. Shared local anchoring is proved within the mesh-group construction and WORLD consumption, without a claim to the full lifetime or publication protocol. Complete world-height bounds, pathological integer overflow, and all admission conditions for sea replacement are outside this coordinate clarification.

The material-type-2 branch of mesher RVA 0xE9530 uses the original four-float constant at RVA 0x43F0E0: 0.25,0.5,0.5,1. Its ordinary water-face vertices use the positive-Z normal for packing and for vertex-light helper RVA 0xE8F70, including the inspected side-face branches. The separately described whole-zone sea quad bypasses that helper. Water type 2 does not take that helper's special type-20 bypass. In the helper, weighted neighboring samples of types 0 or 2 contribute to the unblocked fraction; the unblocked fraction is raised to 1.2. That factor scales the supplied RGB. A weighted RGB luminance using 0.3,0.59,0.11 is capped to 1 by a proportional color rescale when necessary. The helper then adjusts color away from that luminance with multiplier three minus twice the powered unblocked fraction, and clamps RGB to 0 through 1. The helper's fourth result is a weighted mean of sampled illumination bytes divided by 255, using the transparent samples for that average, with a zero result when there is no contributing weight. The final packed fourth color byte therefore carries illumination, not water opacity. The sampling footprint and unrelated mesher generation decisions are not expanded here.

The vertex program computes world position, view position, and homogeneous projected position in that order. The clip-depth offset is subtracted from projected Z; it is zeroed by this pass. It emits projected position both as raster position and as an independent four-component clip-coordinate varying. It emits a view-space normal made by transforming the decoded normal by the world/view linear part and normalizing it. For lighting, it separately transforms and normalizes the normal in world space. Its directional vector is also transformed by the world/view linear part for the pixel-stage light varying, without a further normalization at that output. In the ordinary translation-only water matrices this is the direction in view space. The pixel stage receives a separate view-position vector. These coordinate varyings and lit color use ordinary perspective-correct linear interpolation. The water pixel stage does not read raster SV_POSITION; its screen gradients use the independent clip varying divided by its own W.

Shared vertex lighting first computes a front factor by multiplying the world-normal/directional-light dot product by 0.7, adding 0.3, and flooring at zero. Its back factor is one half plus one half of the absolute normal dot product against the fixed vector approximately 0.5970223,0.39801487,0.69652605. Those vector components have exact bits 0x3F18D674, 0x3ECBC89B, and 0x3F324F88. The front and back colors are weighted by those respective factors, added, and clamped componentwise to 0 through 1. That directional RGB is multiplied by the input COLOR fourth component and material alpha. Ambient RGB is multiplied by the back factor and added separately, without that illumination-byte multiplier.

The vertex stage processes exactly ten point-light slots. A slot contributes only when its radius component is positive. It uses world position minus light position, squared distance divided by squared radius, capped above at 1, then one minus that value as the radial remainder. It normalizes the position-minus-light vector, takes its dot product with the world normal, multiplies by minus 4, and clamps that directional term to 0.3 through 1. The point color RGB is weighted by this term and the square of the radial remainder and accumulated. Color alpha is unused for this contribution. Ambient, illumination-scaled directional light, and summed point light are then combined and clamped componentwise to 0 through 1. Thus point lights enter the shared vertex lighting before the water pixel stage reads lit blue; they are not a separate pixel-stage point-light loop.

The vertex stage doubles input COLOR RGB and computes its luminance with weights 0.299,0.587,0.114. A contrast multiplier is formed by subtracting the combined lighting's green component from ambient alpha, halving that difference, adding 1, and clamping to 1 through 2. Each doubled RGB channel is displaced from its luminance by that multiplier and clamped to 0 through 2. With the pass's glow value zero, this adjusted RGB multiplies combined lighting and material RGB. The whitening scalar blends that result toward RGB 1, followed by a 0-through-1 clamp. The vertex output's fourth color component remains the input COLOR fourth component. The water pixel program consumes only the interpolated blue component of this final lit RGB. It does not consume the lit red, green, or fourth color components for color or opacity. Nonzero-glow alternatives exist in the shared program but are not selected by this water pass and are not specified here.

Pixel constant-buffer slot 0 contains four RGBA rows: first sky color, second sky color, fog color, and sun color. Slot 1 contains fog scale; slot 2 contains the vertical fog/sky translation; slot 3 contains projected sun-reference X and Y, aspect multiplier, and sun-enable scalar; slot 4 contains alpha. RGB components of the four palette rows are the components used here. Setter RVA 0xC7440 uploads those palette rows, and the frame supplies them from renderer offsets 0x4AC, 0x4BC, 0x4CC, and 0x4DC. Setter RVA 0xC6BB0 converts a positive supplied fog distance to its reciprocal fog scale; a nonpositive supplied distance does not update the cached value. Setter RVA 0xC6B10 converts its angle input by subtracting 80 and dividing by minus 30 to form the translation.

The projected sun reference is built by RVA 0xC7650. It scales the supplied direction by 100, applies the view matrix's linear part and the projection matrix, and divides projected coordinates by W. Its stored Y additionally subtracts the angle-input-minus-80 quantity divided by positive 30. Its third component is the supplied width/height ratio. Its fourth component is 1 when the projected depth is within the closed range 0 through 1 and 0 otherwise. The frame's type-2 cell case supplies direction 0,0,minus 1 to this projected-reference setter, while the directional-light setter still receives the frame's directional-light vector. The projected sky-sun reference and the view-space directional-light varying are therefore distinct inputs and must not be substituted for one another. This contract does not expand the frame's time-of-day/environment palette generation.

For the remaining pixel behavior, cubic smoothing means taking three times a scalar's square and subtracting twice its cube. Where a 0-through-1 clamp is stated, it occurs at that point in the sequence. First divide the clip varying's X and Y by its W, then add the slot-2 translation to the divided Y. This translated Y is reused in the following sky and lighting gradients. The first sky interpolation weight is cubic smoothing of the clamped value obtained by multiplying translated Y by 0.4 and adding 0.2. That weight blends first sky color toward second sky color. A lower-sky reference is 0.6 times fog color plus 0.4 times first sky color. For a second weight, take twice translated Y, add 1.5, clamp to 0 through 1, and apply cubic smoothing. This weight blends the lower reference toward the first sky interpolation.

The pixel stage then adds the projected sun effect to that sky reference. Subtract divided clip X from the projected sun X and multiply this horizontal difference by the aspect input. Subtract translated Y from projected sun Y for the vertical difference. Sum the squares of those two differences, multiply by 4, and cap above at 1. Subtract that result from 1 and multiply by the sun-enable scalar. The fourth power of this scalar is the weight blending the current sky reference toward sun RGB. This creates the sky-with-sun color used later in the far-fog blend. It is independent of the view-space reflection term described next.

The illumination palette uses another gradient: add 1 to translated Y, clamp to 0 through 1, apply cubic smoothing, and multiply by 0.8. This weight blends first sky RGB toward second sky RGB. The reflection vector is obtained by taking twice the dot product of the interpolated view-space light vector and interpolated view-space normal, scaling that normal by the result, and subtracting the light vector. Normalize this reflection vector. Normalize view position separately and negate that unit vector to obtain the direction toward the eye. Floor their dot product at zero. The reflection contribution is first sky RGB multiplied by 0.25 and by this nonnegative dot product. The pixel program does not renormalize the incoming normal before constructing the reflection, and it does not use a reflection exponent here. Multiply the illumination palette by interpolated lit blue, add the reflection contribution, and clamp that RGB to 0 through 1.

Distance is squared length of the view-position varying, not clip-space depth, screen-space position, or a world-origin distance. Opacity is computed separately from RGB fog: multiply squared distance by 0.000025, cap above at 1, add 0.75, cap above at 1 again, and multiply by the slot-4 alpha scalar. The literal 0.000025 has exact float bits 0x37D1B717. No vertex color alpha, material alpha, reflection amount, or fog interpolation weight is multiplied into opacity at this pixel stage. Material alpha can still affect lit blue earlier through shared vertex lighting. Pixel RGB is not premultiplied by this opacity.

For RGB fog, multiply squared view distance by the square of fog scale. Form an exponent by taking base-2 exponential of minus 403.954620361328125 times fog scale, multiplying that result by 0.75, and subtracting from 1. The compiled negative coefficient has exact bits 0xC3C9FA31. Raise the distance-times-scale-squared quantity to that exponent through the program's base-2 logarithm/exponential sequence, then cap above at 1 and apply cubic smoothing. Call this the fog weight. First blend the clamped illuminated/reflected RGB toward fog RGB using the fog weight. Then blend that result toward the separately calculated sky-with-sun RGB using the cube of the fog weight, and clamp the resulting RGB to 0 through 1. These two ordered fog blends are distinct operations; replacing them with a single fog-color blend changes the result.

After both fog blends, compute RGB luminance with 0.299,0.587,0.114. Leave red unchanged. Replace green and blue separately by 0.65 of the existing channel plus 0.35 of that luminance. Finally apply cubic smoothing independently to red, green, and blue. This final color curve is after fog and the green/blue tint, not a gamma-2.2 operation applied to the incoming lit color. Opacity is unchanged by these RGB operations. The water pixel blob declares no textures or samplers and performs no texture sampling, scene-color refraction lookup, or time-driven wave displacement. These are statements about this resolved blob, not a census of other rendering paths.
